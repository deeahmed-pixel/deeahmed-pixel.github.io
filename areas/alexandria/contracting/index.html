Contracting company in Alexandria: from land to finishing

Before you sign with a contracting company in Alexandria, know the order of the work and what protects each stage: the soil report before the drawings, the licence before digging, the rebar checked before the pour. Here is what matters when you build over groundwater and near the sea, how the contracting models differ, and how to compare quotes and check the site. Itmam is a finishing and supply company from Alexandria, and we build too: from land to handover under one contract.

Building from land in Alexandria: the right order

Building from land follows a fixed order in which each stage rests on the one before, so a mistake at the start is paid for in everything after it.

  1. Soil report

    Boreholes and a soil report from a specialist before anything is drawn. It tells you the type and depth of foundation, the groundwater level and the salts in the soil. The foundation follows the report, not what was built on the plot next door.

  2. Drawings

    Architectural, structural and MEP drawings from a licensed engineering office. The structural engineer sets the foundations, columns and slabs, the cement type and the concrete cover; the contractor builds to the drawings and does not change them.

  3. Licence

    The building licence comes before digging, not after. What is needed varies with the area, the plot and the type of building, so ask the district office that covers your site for the requirements in writing before you commit to anything, and do not rely on what your neighbour was told.

  4. Excavation and foundations

    Setting out, digging to the level on the drawings, then the blinding concrete, the footings or raft and the tie beams, and waterproofing of the buried concrete as specified.

  5. Concrete frame

    Columns and slabs floor by floor, with the rebar checked before every pour and test cubes taken from every pour.

  6. Walls and roof

    Red brick or block walls, then a roof laid to falls with waterproofing, insulation and a flood test, so the building is watertight before any internal finishing.

  7. Finishing

    Electrical and plumbing first fix, then plaster, floors, gypsum, joinery and paint, as our finishing-steps guide explains.

Groundwater and sea air: what protects the concrete and the steel?

Concrete in Alexandria faces two different threats: salts and groundwater below ground on many sites, and salty air above ground that reaches the steel if the concrete cover is not enough.

  • Sulphate-resisting cement belongs in concrete that touches salty ground or groundwater, such as footings, ground beams and buried tanks, and the decision is the structural engineer’s, based on the soil report. It resists sulphates in the ground, but on its own it does not protect the steel from sea air.
  • Concrete cover is the thickness of concrete between the steel and the outer surface, set by the engineer according to the code and how exposed each element is. On site it is held with concrete spacers under and beside the bars, so the steel never touches the formwork.
  • Dense concrete: no extra water is added on site to make pouring easier, because extra water weakens the concrete and opens its pores, so salt reaches the steel sooner.
  • Sand: washed and clean, and no sea sand in any concrete or mortar with steel in it, because its salts corrode the steel from inside.
  • Rebar storage: on timber bearers off the ground and covered, away from sand and water. A light, tight rust is normal; flaking scale and pitting are not.
  • Digging below the water table: if water appears in the excavation, the engineer decides how it is pumped out and how the foundations are waterproofed before the day of the pour, not on it.

Labour only, labour and materials, or a management fee?

The contracting model decides who buys the material and who carries any change in its price, so understand it before you compare any figure.

ModelHow it worksIts advantageWatch out for
Labour onlyYou buy the materials; the contractor provides the labour and the workYou choose the material and its source yourselfBuying, quantities, waste and dates are on you, and any late material stops the site
Labour and materials (lump sum)One price per item or for the whole building; the contractor buys everythingYou know the total from the startA vague specification lets lesser material in, and the contractor prices any change
Management feeYou pay each supplier directly at their price; the contractor takes an agreed feeInvoices in your name and no hidden margin on materialsWhat the fee covers, and what it does not, must be written down clearly

Ours is the third model: you pay suppliers directly with the tax invoice in your name, and our fee is agreed in writing before we begin. Our finishing-contract guide explains the clauses in any contract to check before you sign.

How to compare contractors’ quotes: the BOQ line by line

Compare quotes on one bill of quantities (BOQ) with the same items, quantities and specifications, because a total on its own does not tell you what you will receive.

  • The BOQ: every item with its description, unit, quantity and specification, not a line that says “complete shell”.
  • Concrete: the strength required by the drawings, the cement type in the foundations and in the rest of the frame, and whether it is ready-mix or mixed on site.
  • Rebar: the diameters and grade on the drawings, the quantity from the bending schedule, and who provides the mill test certificate.
  • What each item includes: digging and removing the spoil, pumping out water, formwork, waterproofing, cube tests and debris.
  • Quotes per square metre: if the quote is a rate per metre of floor area, ask exactly what it includes and what is charged separately, because two quotes with the same name can differ in half their items.
  • Payments: tied to stages that are finished and checked, not to dates on the calendar.
  • Exclusions: everything the quote does not cover, written by name, so it does not surprise you halfway through.

A lower figure can mean a missing item or a lesser specification, so ask why the quotes differ before you choose. Our BOQ guide explains each line.

Site supervision: what to check before and after every pour

The most important moment in a concrete frame is the time before the pour, because once concrete is poured it can only be checked by breaking it.

  1. Rebar before the pour

    Diameters, numbers, spacing, lap lengths and stirrups match the drawings, the bars are clean of mud and oil, and the spacers are in place. The pour does not start until the supervising engineer has approved the reinforcement.

  2. Formwork

    Clean and tight so no cement slurry leaks out, plumb and well propped, with the openings for pipes in the slab set before the pour, not broken out afterwards.

  3. During the pour

    The ready-mix delivery ticket matches what was ordered, a slump test, test cubes taken, vibration without overdoing it, and no water added to the mix on site.

  4. Curing

    The concrete is kept damp by spraying or covering for several days after the pour, especially in heat and wind, because fast drying causes cracks and weakens the surface. The curing period and striking the formwork are the engineer’s decision.

  5. Cube results

    Cubes are usually crushed at 7 and 28 days, and the results are kept in writing with the rebar certificates in the building’s file.

A shell standing for years, or an extra floor: check before you continue

A frame that has stood unfinished for years, or a building you plan to build on, is checked before it is continued, because concrete exposed to sea air can suffer without it showing from a distance.

  • Starter bars: the column steel standing above the roof; if it is badly rusted or pitted, a structural engineer decides how it is treated or extended.
  • The concrete: cracks running along the bars, or flaking pieces, are signs of rust inside, and are assessed before anything is built on top.
  • Tests: the engineer decides what is needed, such as taking concrete cores, and writes a report on what needs repair or strengthening.
  • An extra floor is built only after a structural check of what will carry it, and after confirming with the district office that the floor is allowed and licensed. Extra load on a building that was not designed for it puts every resident at risk.

With us: we survey what is there, test the concrete and price the repairs clearly before we continue.

Questions people ask

How do I choose a contracting company in Alexandria?
Choose on the BOQ and the supervision, not on the total. Ask for a written, itemised BOQ with quantities and specifications, ask who buys the material and whose name is on the invoice, how they check the rebar before a pour and hand over the cube results, and visit sites they are working on. A quote with no BOQ and no specification is hard to hold anyone to later.
Where does building a residential block start?
With the soil report, not with digging. Then the structural and architectural drawings from an engineering office, then the licence from the district, then excavation, foundations, frame, walls and roof waterproofing, and finishing last. An owner who skips the soil report to save time can end up paying for a different foundation later.
Is a labour-only builder better than one who supplies materials?
It depends on your time and your buying experience. With labour only you choose the material yourself, but the quantities, waste and delivery dates are on you. With materials included you know the total from the start, but the specification must be written in detail. We work a third way: you pay each supplier directly at their price with the invoice in your name, and our management fee is agreed in writing.
Do foundations in Alexandria need sulphate-resisting cement?
If the soil report shows salts or groundwater in contact with the concrete, usually yes, and the decision is the structural engineer’s, in the specification. It resists the sulphates in the ground, but on its own it does not protect the steel from sea air: that is the job of the concrete cover, dense concrete and washed sand.
Are you a contracting and supply company? Can you supply just the rebar and cement?
Yes, supply-only is available. We supply cement, rebar, bricks and blocks, and sand if you have your own contractor; you get a written quotation for exactly your order, and you pay the supplier with the invoice in your name. If you want the whole build, we build from land to handover under one contract.
How much does building cost per square metre?
There is no honest figure without drawings and a soil report. The cost depends on the foundations, the floor area and number of floors, the cement type and rebar grade and their prices on the day, the walls and waterproofing, and the finishing level. We visit, measure and give you a written, itemised quotation; you pay suppliers directly at their price, and our management fee is agreed in writing.
