Aluminium windows in Alexandria: supply and installation

Choosing aluminium windows in Alexandria starts with the place: salt air, wind on high floors, street noise and winter condensation. Here is what matters in the specification, how to replace old timber windows, how to measure, and what a quote must list before you agree to it.

Get a quotationWhatsApp

What is different about aluminium windows in Alexandria?

A window in Alexandria faces four things: sea salt that attacks metal and coatings, strong wind on high floors facing the sea, noise from busy streets, and humidity that condenses on the glass in winter.

On top of that, many older buildings still have timber windows and shutters that have lost their seal, letting in dust, salt air and rain. So it is not enough to ask about the colour of the aluminium; ask about the coating, the fixings, the glass, the drainage and how it will be installed.

In Egypt the word for aluminium joinery is also used for aluminium kitchens, which are a different product; our kitchen materials guide compares them with other materials. This page is about windows, doors, shutters and glazing.

Sea air: coating, fixings and drainage

Near the sea the metal, the coating and the hardware suffer before the glass does, so the coastal specification is set from the start, not after installation.

PartWhat happens near the seaWhat to ask for
CoatingAn ordinary coating blisters and peels, usually starting at cut edges and scratchesSeaside-class powder coating over proper pre-treatment
Screws and fixingsOrdinary steel rusts and stains the frame and the wall316 stainless steel screws and fixings
HardwareOrdinary hinges, locks and rollers rust and seizeStainless or marine-grade hardware
DrainageSalt water trapped inside the profile corrodes it from withinDrainage slots inside the profiles, kept open and clear of sand and dust
GasketsLow-grade gaskets dry out and crack in the sun, letting in air and waterGaskets that cope with the sun, replaced when they harden

After installation, rinse the frames with fresh water from time to time to remove the salt that builds up, and clean the drainage slots and sliding tracks.

Double glazing for noise, heat and condensation

Double glazing, two panes with a sealed gap between them, reduces heat, noise and condensation, but how well the window seals matters as much as the glass.

  • Noise: double glazing cuts street and sea noise, and laminated acoustic glass or two panes of different thicknesses cut it further. But a sliding window that does not seal lets sound in around the sash whatever the glass, and a casement window closes tighter against its gaskets.
  • Heat: the gap between the panes slows the heat passing through, and solar-control glass reduces the sun coming in, which eases the load on the air conditioning on sunny elevations.
  • Condensation: a window that “sweats” on the inside in winter is warm, humid indoor air meeting cold glass. Double glazing keeps the inner pane warmer so there is less condensation, a thermal-break profile reduces it on the frame, and ventilation and extractor fans reduce the humidity at its source.
  • If mist appears between the two panes, the unit has lost its seal and needs replacing; wiping the outside will not help.

High floors facing the sea: wind and opening size

The higher the floor, the bigger the opening and the more directly it faces the sea, the deeper the profile and the more reinforcement the window needs, and that is decided for the actual opening, not from a catalogue.

  • Profile depth and reinforcement are chosen for the width and height of the opening and how exposed it is to wind. A very wide sliding sash on a high floor may need a heavier system, or splitting into more sashes.
  • Glass thickness is set by the pane size, and large panes or panes close to the floor need toughened or laminated safety glass.
  • The frame is fixed into the concrete or brickwork with stainless screws at suitable spacing, not into the plaster alone.
  • Wind drives rain into the tracks, so the window needs proper drainage and tight gaskets, and doors facing the sea need a suitable threshold.
  • Safety: in homes with children, ask about sash locks and opening restrictors on high floors.

Replacing old timber windows, and enclosing a balcony

In an older building the window is part of a façade every resident shares, so the look and the decision are not yours alone.

  • Before replacing: is the timber worth restoring? A sound frame and solid sashes can be repaired and repainted; timber that is rotten or has lost its shape is replaced.
  • The façade: choose a colour and division close to the building’s other windows, and ask the building management or the owners’ association before changing the colour or shape. Some older Alexandria buildings have architectural value, so ask the district office too before any visible change to the façade.
  • Removal and installation: an old opening is rarely square, so the size is checked once you know how the old frame is fixed. After installation the gaps are filled and sealed outside with a waterproof sealant, and the plaster around the opening is made good and painted.
  • Shutters: old timber shutters can be restored or replaced with aluminium shutters, hinged, sliding or roller, with the same coastal coating and fixings.
  • Enclosing a balcony changes the façade, so check with the building management and the district office before you order anything. Make sure the balcony floor drains and is waterproofed, that the balcony wall can carry the glass, and that some sashes open for ventilation. If there is a gas water heater on the balcony, enclosing it reduces the ventilation it needs, so ask the gas company first.

How to measure a window before asking for a quote

Your measurements are enough for a first quote; the final size is taken on site before anything is made.

  1. Width in three places

    Measure the width of the opening from wall to wall at the top, middle and bottom, and note the smallest.

  2. Height in three places

    Measure the height on the left, in the middle and on the right, and again note the smallest.

  3. The diagonals

    Measure from corner to opposite corner both ways. If the diagonals differ noticeably, the opening is out of square, which is common in older buildings.

  4. Depth and shutters

    Note the wall depth, whether there is a shutter or a shutter box, and which way the window opens.

  5. Photos and location

    Photograph the opening inside and out, and note the floor and whether it faces the sea or a busy street.

What should an aluminium quote list?

Compare aluminium quotes item by item, because two quotes for the same window can differ in profile, glass, hardware and what each one includes.

  • The profile system: casement or sliding, the wall thickness of the profile, and whether it has a thermal break.
  • The glass: single or double, the thickness of each pane and the gap, clear, solar-control or acoustic, and where safety glass is used.
  • The hardware: hinges, locks, rollers and handles, what they are made of, and whether they suit sea air.
  • Colour and coating: the colour, the coating type, and whether it is seaside class.
  • Fixings and sealing: the screws and whether they are 316 stainless, and the sealant around the frame.
  • Mosquito nets: fixed, sliding or pleated, and on which sashes.
  • Shutters: the type, manual or motorised, and where the box goes.
  • What is included: removing the old windows, taking away the debris, and making good and painting around the opening.
  • How it is counted: by the square metre or by the opening, and what each includes.

A quote that says “first-grade aluminium” with no system, thickness or glass type is hard to check at handover. Our finishing quotation guide explains how to read any quote line by line.

Questions people ask

What are the best aluminium windows for a seafront flat in Alexandria?
No single name is best. What matters is seaside-class powder coating, 316 stainless fixings, hardware that copes with salt, drainage inside the profile, and a profile and reinforcement matched to the opening and the floor. If noise is a problem, add double glazing and a casement window that closes tightly.
Does double glazing really stop noise?
It reduces it a lot, but not on its own. Sound gets in around the sash if the window does not seal, so a casement window closes tighter than a sliding one. On a busy street, ask about laminated acoustic glass or two panes of different thicknesses.
Why does my window sweat on the inside in winter?
That is condensation: warm, humid air inside the home meeting cold glass. Double glazing and a thermal-break profile reduce it, and ventilation and extractor fans in the kitchen and bathroom cut the humidity at its source. If the mist is between the two panes, the unit itself has lost its seal and needs replacing.
Aluminium or timber shutters?
Near the sea, aluminium shutters with a seaside-class coating do not rot or crack and need less upkeep. Timber looks warmer and suits older façades, but needs repainting and care from time to time. In an older building, keep the look and colour close to the rest of the façade.
Can I enclose my balcony with aluminium and glass?
Ask first: enclosing a balcony changes the façade, so check with the building management and the district office before you order. Then make sure the floor drains and is waterproofed, leave some sashes that open for ventilation, and if there is a gas water heater on the balcony, ask the gas company before you enclose it.
How much does aluminium cost per square metre?
It depends on the profile system and its thickness, the glass, the hardware, the colour and coating, the nets and shutters, and the size of the opening.
