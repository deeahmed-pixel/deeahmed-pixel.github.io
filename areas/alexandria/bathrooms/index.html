Bathroom finishing and renovation in Alexandria

Bathroom finishing and renovation in Alexandria starts with what is under the tiles, not their colour: the waterproofing, the pipes, the falls and the ventilation. Here is when a refresh without stripping is enough and when the room must go back to the slab, how to plan a small bathroom, and what protects the flat below.

Get a quotationWhatsApp

What is different about a bathroom in Alexandria?

A bathroom in Alexandria faces three things at once: older buildings whose pipes and waterproofing have reached the end of their life, high humidity all year round, and small bathrooms in flats that were never planned for today’s fittings.

The most common complaint is water coming through the ceiling of the flat below. The cause is usually failed waterproofing under the tiles, or old pipes leaking inside the wall or floor. Many buildings still have galvanised steel supply pipes, which rust from the inside, so the pressure drops, the water runs discoloured, and then the joints start to leak.

Damp shows up as black spots in the ceiling corners, especially in a bathroom whose window opens onto a narrow light well, or that has no window. So any renovation starts with a survey: where the water comes from, what state the pipes are in, and how the steam gets out.

Renovating a bathroom without demolition: when it works and when it does not

A bathroom can be refreshed without stripping it only if what lies under the tiles is sound, and nobody can promise that before checking the room.

ConditionRefresh without strippingMust go back to the slab
LeaksNo sign of water on the neighbour’s ceiling or in the adjoining wallsA stain or seepage at the neighbour’s, or in an adjoining wall
TilesFirm and flat, with no hollow sound when tappedTiles that sound hollow, are cracked, or sit on loose mortar
PipesAlready replaced with PPR and in good conditionOld galvanised steel, or leaks at the joints
LevelsA new layer will not raise the threshold or stop the door opening, and the floor already falls to the drainWater stands on the floor, or there is no height for a new layer
ChangesThe WC, basin and shower stay where they areMoving the WC or the drain, or fitting a concealed cistern and concealed mixers

A refresh usually means new sanitaryware and exposed mixers, a new vanity and mirror, fresh grout and silicone, and sometimes new tiles laid over firmly bonded old ones with a suitable adhesive. It does not fix failed waterproofing or old pipes inside the wall: tiling over a problem hides it, it does not solve it. Before deciding, we open a small inspection area and show you what is under the tiles.

A full bathroom renovation, step by step

Fully renovating an old bathroom means stripping back to the concrete slab and renewing everything under the tiles, and it is the only dependable fix once water has reached the neighbours.

  1. Strip back to the slab

    The tiles, the old mortar and the old pipes come out. In some older buildings the pipes run through a layer of fill under the floor; if the fill is saturated, it is removed and replaced.

  2. Pipes and drains

    PPR supply pipes replace galvanised steel, PVC drains run with enough fall to the building’s stack, the bathroom gets its own shut-off valve within easy reach, and the pipes are pressure-tested before the wall is closed. If the shared drain stack itself leaks, that is agreed with the building management.

  3. Electrics

    The water heater circuit, sockets, lighting and extractor points go in before plastering and tiling, on circuits protected by an earth-leakage breaker.

  4. Falls and waterproofing

    The floor is set to fall towards the drain so no water stands, and a full waterproofing tank covers the floor and turns up the walls, higher in the shower area, with the corners, the drain and the pipes reinforced.

  5. Flood test

    The drain is blocked and the floor flooded for a day or two while the ceiling of the flat below is checked, so arrange this with your neighbour in advance. With us, the test is recorded with photos before any tile goes down.

  6. Tiles and fittings

    The tiles are laid to the falls, then the vanity, sanitaryware, mixers and glass go in.

  7. Handover

    Run all the taps together, fill the shower floor and check that it drains with no water standing, and look under the basin and around the WC before you sign.

Damp and mould: light well or extractor fan?

Steam has to leave the bathroom; if it does not, it condenses on the ceiling and walls and mould appears, however good the paint.

  • A window onto the light well helps if it actually opens and the light well is open at the top. Do not rely on a narrow, closed light well alone.
  • An extractor fan ducted outside or into the light well, wired to run with the light or left on for a while after a shower. If the duct would come out on the building’s façade, ask the building management first.
  • A bathroom with no window: here the extractor is a necessity, not a luxury, with a gap under the door to let air in.
  • The ceiling: a moisture-resistant, washable paint, and for a suspended ceiling, moisture-resistant gypsum board with an access panel. Existing mould is cleaned and treated, but it comes back if the cause stays.
  • The vanity unit: a carcass in moisture-resistant board with every edge sealed, because an open edge is the first thing to swell.

If the damp is in a shared wall or rising from the ground, that is a different problem from steam, and our guide to damp walls explains the difference.

Small bathrooms in older flats: a layout that makes room

In a small bathroom the layout matters more than any material: where the WC sits, how the door opens, and where the shower water goes.

  • A shower instead of a bath saves space, and a toughened glass screen instead of a curtain keeps the water in and lets the light through, so the room feels bigger.
  • The door: if it hits the WC or basin as it opens inwards, consider a door that opens outwards or a sliding door.
  • A wall-hung WC with a concealed cistern saves a little depth and makes cleaning easier, but it needs a wall that can take the cistern, so decide before demolition, not after.
  • The WC position: it is best kept close to the drain stack, because moving it far away needs a fall the floor may not allow.
  • A wall-hung vanity with drawers, a mirror cabinet, and a recessed niche if the wall is thick enough: storage that does not eat the floor.
  • On the walls, larger tiles in a light colour with fewer joints; on the floor, a size that can follow the falls to the drain.
  • The washing machine: in many older flats it lives in the bathroom, so plan its drain, valve and a protected socket from the start.

Water heaters and electrics near water

Water and electricity in a small room need planning from the start, not connections added after the finishes are on.

  • An earth-leakage breaker on the bathroom circuits and a sound earth. Many older Alexandria buildings have no such breaker and still have aluminium wiring, so test the electrics before adding a new heater.
  • An electric storage heater: on a wall that can carry its full weight, away from the shower spray, on a circuit suited to its rating, where it can be reached for maintenance, and with its safety valve piped to the drain.
  • A gas water heater does not go inside a closed bathroom, because it needs ventilation and a flue to the outside. Any change to its position or to the gas line is a matter for the gas company.
  • Sockets and switches: away from the shower and basin, with covers where they could be splashed.
  • Lighting: fittings made for wet areas, especially above the shower and inside niches.

Finishes: tiles, the vanity top, and what super lux means

Choose bathroom materials for water first: a porcelain floor with a slip rating suited to wet floors, porcelain or ceramic on the walls, and epoxy grout in the shower, because it does not absorb water and resists stains and mould better than cement grout.

Choose the vanity top while looking at the stone itself at our marble and granite showroom in El Deresa, Moharram Bey. Granite copes with scratches and water, quartz does not absorb liquids, and marble has the finest veining but marks with acidic cleaners. Vanity units and stone tops are made in our own workshops, and the stone is templated, cut and installed by the same team.

A “super lux” bathroom is not a fixed specification, and the term changes from one contractor to the next, so ask for every item to be written into the quotation by name and specification: tiles, sanitaryware, mixers, cistern, shower screen, ceiling and extractor.

If the kitchen needs work too, doing the bathroom and kitchen together is usually easier to organise: the pipes are renewed once, the water is shut off once, and the demolition happens once.

Questions people ask

How do I finish a small bathroom in an older flat?
Start with the layout: a shower with a glass screen instead of a bath, the WC close to the drain stack, a wall-hung vanity with drawers, and a door that does not hit the WC. Before any tiles, replace the old pipes, waterproof and flood test, and fit an extractor if the light well is narrow or there is no window.
Can I renovate a bathroom without demolition?
Sometimes: if there is no leak at the neighbour’s, the tiles are firm and do not sound hollow, the pipes have already been replaced and the levels allow a new layer. Then you can change the sanitaryware, mixers, vanity and grout, or tile over the old tiles. If there is a leak or old steel pipes, stripping back to the slab is the dependable fix.
My bathroom is leaking into the flat below. What do I do?
Find the source first: a supply pipe, a drain, failed waterproofing under the tiles, or the joints around the bath or shower. If water comes through even when the bathroom is not being used, it is usually a supply pipe. The dependable fix is usually to lift the tiles, replace the pipes, waterproof again and flood test before tiling.
What does a super lux bathroom mean?
The term has no fixed specification. It usually means good porcelain up to the ceiling over tested waterproofing, better sanitaryware and mixers, and sometimes a concealed cistern, a glass shower screen, a moisture-resistant suspended ceiling and an extractor. What matters is that all of it is written into the quotation item by item, by name and specification.
Should I do the bathroom and kitchen together?
Usually yes, if both need work: the pipes are renewed once, the water is shut off once, and the demolition and debris happen once. You can also choose the kitchen worktop and the vanity top on the same visit to our El Deresa showroom.
How much does bathroom renovation cost in Alexandria?
It depends on the size of the bathroom, whether it goes back to the slab, the state of the pipes, the tiles, sanitaryware and mixers, and the vanity and its top. We do not price a bathroom we have not seen: we visit, measure and give you a written, itemised quotation; you pay suppliers directly at their price, and our management fee is agreed in writing.
