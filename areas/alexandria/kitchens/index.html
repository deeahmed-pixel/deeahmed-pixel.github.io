Kitchens in Alexandria: design, manufacture and installation

A kitchen in Alexandria lives with sea-air humidity, and many sit in older flats with narrow rooms and pipes and wiring that need checking. Here is what makes the difference in an Alexandrian kitchen, and how the work runs from measuring to installation. Itmam makes kitchens in its own workshops and installs them, and you choose the worktop at our showroom in El Deresa, Moharram Bey.

Get a quotationWhatsApp

What is different about a kitchen in Alexandria?

Three things set an Alexandria kitchen apart: humidity and sea air, the many older buildings with narrow kitchens and old services, and work inside occupied buildings.

Moisture reaches the cabinets from the air, from cooking steam and from around the sink, and in flats near the Corniche the salt air corrodes ordinary steel hinges and handles. An older flat may hide corroded pipes behind the kitchen wall, or wiring that cannot carry today’s appliances. And an occupied building has its own hours, a lift and neighbours, and long pieces have to go up the stairs or fit in the lift.

So a good kitchen does not start with the door colour. It starts with a survey: the real measurements, the state of the pipes and wiring, the ventilation, and the route the pieces will take into the flat.

Small kitchens in older flats: making the space work

In a narrow kitchen the layout matters more than any material, because it decides the worktop, the storage and the room to move.

LayoutSuitsWatch for
Single wallA very narrow kitchen, or one in a corridorLittle worktop, so use the height with wall cabinets up to the ceiling
Galley, on two facing wallsA long, narrow kitchen, common in older flatsLeave a walkway wide enough to open doors and drawers and stand in front of them at the same time
L-shape, on two adjoining wallsA squarer kitchen, or one with a window or door in one wallThe corner is wasted unless it gets a proper corner fitting
U-shape, on three wallsA slightly wider kitchenIn a narrow room the walkway feels cramped and the two corners are awkward

In older buildings with tall ceilings, a second row of wall cabinets can take things you rarely use. Drawers in the base units use the depth better than shelves, and a tall unit that combines the fridge and oven saves worktop. If the washing machine lives in the kitchen, as it does in many flats, fix its position, size and drain from the start, not after the cabinets are made. A modern kitchen with plain fronts and recessed handles or a handleless profile looks calmer in a small space, and leaves nothing sticking out into a narrow walkway.

Behind the kitchen wall: pipes, power and gas before cabinets

In an older flat, check what is behind the kitchen wall before any cabinet goes up, because taking a new kitchen apart to fix a pipe is harder and costlier than fixing the pipe first.

  • Pipes: old, corroded pipes under the sink and inside the wall are replaced before installation, and the new ones are pressure-tested before the wall is closed.
  • Drainage: moving the sink away from its original place needs enough fall to the building’s drain stack, so check the route before the design is approved.
  • Power: many older Alexandria buildings have aluminium wiring and boards that have been extended again and again. The oven, an electric hob, the dishwasher, the washing machine and the water heater each need a suitable circuit, so test the wiring before adding new appliances.
  • Gas: any change to the natural gas line is a matter for the gas company, so ask them before you settle on a new position for the cooker.
  • Extraction: a hood ducted to the outside clears steam, grease and moisture. If the kitchen looks onto a light well or the building’s façade, ask the building management where the duct may come out.

Mark sockets and water points on the kitchen drawing itself, not by eye on the wall, so that no socket ends up behind a cabinet side or under the worktop.

Materials that survive Alexandria’s humidity

Humidity does not hit the whole kitchen equally. It gathers in known places, and protecting those places matters more than the name of the door material.

WhereWhat happensWhat to do
Sink cabinetA small leak or drip reaches a board edge and it swellsA carcass in moisture-resistant board, every edge sealed with edge banding, and a liner protecting the cabinet floor
Beside the dishwasher and washing machineHot steam when opened, and water if anything failsSealed edges, a protective strip under the worktop above the dishwasher, and a shut-off valve within easy reach
Above the cookerSteam, grease and heatA hood ducted outside, and wall-cabinet doors that cope with steam and wipe clean
Hardware near the seaSalt air rusts ordinary steelHinges, runners and handles with a finish made for sea air in flats close to the Corniche
An outside wall or a light-well wallCondensation and damp from the wall itselfTreat the damp first, and leave a small ventilation gap behind the cabinet

The full comparison of HPL, acrylic, lacquer, wood and aluminium is in our kitchen materials guide. The Alexandria rule is simple: a good front on a weak board or with open edges will not last long.

The worktop: see the stone at our El Deresa showroom

Choose the worktop while looking at the stone itself, not a photo on a phone, because colour and veining look different in person and under kitchen lighting.

At our marble and granite showroom in El Deresa, Moharram Bey, you can compare granite, quartz, and Egyptian and imported marble, and compare polished with honed finishes. Bring a sample of the door colour and the kitchen measurements, so you choose the stone and the cabinets as one set. What is on show changes, so send us the name of the stone or the look you want before you come.

In short: granite copes with heat and scratches, quartz does not absorb liquids but needs a trivet under hot pans, and marble marks with lemon and vinegar. Our marble, granite and quartz guide compares them in detail.

  • Thickness and edge profile: agreed before cutting.
  • Sink cut-out: undermount or top-mounted, and where the tap goes.
  • Hob or cooker cut-out: to the actual appliance, so settle the model before cutting.
  • Joint positions: in a long kitchen, or if a slab will not go up the stairs or in the lift in one piece, the joints are set before cutting and kept away from the sink where possible.

The worktop is templated after the cabinets are fixed and levelled, so it fits the real kitchen, not the drawing. With us, the same team templates, cuts and installs the stone.

How a kitchen job runs, from survey to installation

  1. Survey and measuring

    We measure the whole kitchen: walls, height, window, doors and the positions of pipes, gas and power, and we check the walls for damp. We also measure the flat’s front door, the stairs and the lift, because the pieces have to get in.

  2. Layout, appliances and materials

    We agree the layout, where the sink, cooker, fridge and washing machine go, and the appliance models with their sizes, because the cut-outs are drawn around them; then you choose the carcass, fronts, hardware and stone, so the colours are approved together.

  3. Drawing and written quotation

    You receive the kitchen drawing and a written, itemised quotation: cabinets, fronts, hardware, worktop, and plumbing and electrical changes, each as its own item. Nothing is made until the drawing is approved.

  4. Preparing the room

    The pipes and wiring that need it are replaced, damp is treated, and the wall and floor are prepared before the cabinets arrive.

  5. Making in the workshop

    The cabinets are made in our workshops to the approved drawing, which means less cutting and dust inside the building.

  6. Cabinets first, then the worktop

    The cabinets are fitted and levelled, then the worktop is templated on the real kitchen, cut and installed, and finally the sink and appliances are connected.

  7. Handover

    Open every door and drawer, check that the fronts and joints line up, run the water and look under the sink for leaks before you sign.

How to compare kitchen quotes in Alexandria

Compare quotes item by item, not by the bottom line, because two quotes for the same kitchen can differ in material, size and what each one includes.

  • Carcass and fronts: the board type and thickness, whether it is moisture-resistant with every edge sealed, and the door material and finish.
  • Hardware: the type of hinges, runners and lift systems, their load rating, and whether they suit sea air if the flat is near the water.
  • Worktop: the stone, its thickness and edge, the number and position of joints, and the sink and hob cut-outs.
  • What is included: removing the old kitchen and the debris, the sink and tap, plumbing and electrical changes, the splashback between the cabinets, and the installation itself.
  • What is excluded: usually the appliances, and any work on the gas line, so ask for that in writing.
  • How it is counted: some quotes go by the length of the kitchen and some by the unit, so ask what each includes so you compare like with like.

A quote that does not name the materials and their thickness is hard to check at handover. Our finishing quotation guide explains how to read any quote line by line.

Questions people ask

How do I plan a small kitchen in an older flat?
Start with the layout, not the material: in a long, narrow kitchen a galley or single-wall layout usually works better than a U-shape on three walls, use the height with cabinets up to the ceiling, and fix the washing machine and fridge positions early. Check the pipes and wiring before the cabinets go in.
What kitchen material copes best with Alexandria’s humidity?
The carcass and the edges matter more than the door: moisture-resistant board, every edge sealed, a protected sink cabinet and a hood that vents outside. On that basis HPL, acrylic and lacquer all last well, aluminium is not affected by humidity, and solid wood is the most sensitive. Near the sea, choose hardware with a finish made for sea air.
Where can I see worktop stone in Alexandria before choosing?
At our marble and granite showroom in El Deresa, Moharram Bey, Saturday to Thursday, 09:00 to 18:00. What is on show changes, so WhatsApp us on +20 106 926 2572 with the stone or look you want before you come, and bring a door-colour sample and the kitchen measurements.
Can I move the sink or the cooker?
The sink usually can, if the drain can reach the building’s stack with enough fall, which we check at the survey. The cooker depends on the gas line, and any change to it is a matter for the gas company, so ask them before you approve the design.
How long does a kitchen take from measuring to installation?
It depends on the size of the kitchen, the materials you choose and their availability, how quickly the drawing and appliances are approved, and any pipes or wiring that must be replaced first. The worktop is templated after the cabinets are fitted, so it is the last step. We go through the timing with you after the survey.
How much does a kitchen cost in Alexandria?
It depends on the length and layout, the carcass and door materials, the hardware, the stone and its thickness, and the plumbing and electrical changes. We do not price a kitchen we have not seen: we visit, measure and give you a written, itemised quotation; you pay suppliers directly at their price, and our management fee is agreed in writing.
