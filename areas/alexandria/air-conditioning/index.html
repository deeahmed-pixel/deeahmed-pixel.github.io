AC installation in Alexandria: first fix, installation and commissioning

Installing air conditioning in Alexandria means dealing with salty sea air, high humidity all year round, and older buildings whose wiring was never sized for today’s appliances. Here is how air conditioning is first-fixed before the gypsum and paint, how split and concealed units differ, what decides a unit’s capacity, where the outdoor unit should go, and how to check the work and look after the system.

First-fixing air conditioning during finishing, before gypsum and paint

Air conditioning is first-fixed before the gypsum, plaster and paint, because the copper pipes, drains and power disappear inside walls and ceilings, and any change afterwards means breaking out and repainting.

  1. Positions first

    The positions of the indoor and outdoor units, the pipe and drain routes and the power point for each unit are marked on the drawing and on the wall. An indoor unit should not blow straight onto a bed or sofa or be blocked by a curtain or wardrobe, and its air should be thrown along the length of the room.

  2. Type and capacity before the first fix

    Even if you will buy the units later, decide the type and approximate capacity for each room now, because the copper pipe sizes and the maximum route length vary from one unit to another under the maker’s instructions.

  3. Copper pipes

    Two copper lines for each indoor unit, each with its own insulation, inside the wall or in a gypsum board bulkhead, on the shortest route with the fewest bends, with the interconnecting cable in conduit. The ends stay capped until installation day, and the pipes are pressure tested and vacuumed before the refrigerant is charged.

  4. The drain line

    An insulated drain pipe with a fall of at least 1:100, that is 1 cm in every metre, ending at a real outlet: a floor drain or a drain stack with a trap, never a ceiling void or open air.

  5. A circuit for every unit

    A dedicated circuit from the board for each unit, with its own labelled breaker, cable sized for the unit’s rating and the length of the run, and a power point next to the unit, not a spur off the nearest socket.

  6. Gypsum and paint, then the units

    Then the gypsum is closed and the walls painted, and the units go in at the end, away from sanding dust. Concealed units get an access hatch at every unit.

Split or concealed air conditioning?

A split unit is simpler to install, service and replace; a concealed ducted unit is invisible in the room and quieter, but needs the ceiling coordinated from the first drawing.

ItemWall-mounted splitConcealed ducted
LookA visible unit on the wall in every roomOnly linear or square grilles in the ceiling
CeilingNo suspended ceiling neededNeeds depth in the suspended ceiling, coordinated with lighting and beams from the start
ServicingThe filter is within reach, and replacing one unit leaves the rest aloneAn access hatch at every unit, and the filter is cleaned through it
ControlEach room at its own temperatureRooms served by one unit usually share one temperature
Best forExisting flats, room-by-room control, and fitting into a finished flatReceptions and rooms where you do not want a visible unit, in a flat being finished

You can mix the two: concealed in the reception, splits in the bedrooms. With a multi-split, one outdoor unit serves several indoor units.

Choosing the capacity: the room, its orientation and the sun

A unit’s capacity is worked out for the room itself, not from its floor area alone, so two rooms of the same size can need different capacities.

  • Orientation and sun: a west-facing room takes the afternoon sun, large glazing facing the sun adds load, and double or solar-control glazing reduces it.
  • The floor: a top-floor flat under the roof gets hotter, especially if the roof is not thermally insulated.
  • Height: the high ceilings of older Alexandria buildings mean more air to cool.
  • Use: how many people are usually there, appliances that give off heat, and a kitchen open to the reception.
  • Bigger is not better: an oversized unit cools quickly and switches off before it has removed the moisture, leaving the room cold and clammy at the same time, which you notice in Alexandria’s humidity. An undersized one runs all the time and never reaches the set temperature.

So we calculate the load for each room and tell you what capacity we arrived at and why, instead of picking a size from an area chart.

The outdoor unit: sea air, the façade and condensate

The outdoor unit is the first thing to suffer near the sea, and the first thing neighbours complain about if it is badly fixed or drained.

  • Sea air: near the Corniche, salt eats the coil fins and the fixings, so we write anti-corrosion coated coils and stainless fixings into the quotation. If you are buying the unit yourself, ask the supplier about coastal protection for the coil before you buy.
  • Brackets: galvanised or stainless and rated for the unit’s weight, fixed into concrete or brickwork, not plaster, with stainless bolts, anti-vibration rubber pads, and clearance around the unit for airflow.
  • The façade: many buildings have rules on where outdoor units go, in the light well, on the roof or on a service balcony rather than the main façade, so ask the building management or the owners’ association before the first fix. The pipes run inside the wall, not clipped across the façade.
  • Narrow light wells: an outdoor unit in a narrow, enclosed light well draws its own hot air back in and cools less, so check the clearance the unit’s instructions ask for.
  • Condensate: it must not drip onto the neighbours’ balcony or the pavement. The drain line runs to the balcony floor drain or to the drain stack with a trap, and where there is no natural fall, a small condensate pump is fitted with the unit.

Electrics in older buildings: can they take the air conditioning?

Test the electrics before adding any air conditioning to an older building, because a lot of Alexandria wiring is still aluminium, on boards extended again and again.

  • Aluminium wiring and old joints heat up under the steady load of air conditioning, especially with coastal humidity at the terminations; replacing them is a safety job first and a convenience second. And never run air conditioning off an extension lead or a shared socket.
  • An earth-leakage breaker and a sound earth, which many older buildings lack.
  • The meter’s capacity: add up the load of every unit, the water heater and the kitchen before you buy, because increasing the supply needs an application to the electricity company and takes time.
  • With us, we open the board, test it and give you a written finding, and if replacing part of the wiring is genuinely enough, we tell you so.

How to check the installation at handover

Do not sign for the handover until every unit has run in front of you, its drain has been tested and its readings written down.

  1. Pressure test and vacuum

    Ask when the pipes were pressure tested and vacuumed before the refrigerant was charged, and ask for it to be recorded.

  2. Running and readings

    Run every unit on cooling: the air should be strong and even, and the temperature difference between the air going into the unit and the air coming out is measured and written on the handover sheet with the refrigerant charge.

  3. Drain test

    Water is poured into the indoor unit’s tray and followed to the end of the line, with no dripping from the unit.

  4. Insulation and openings

    Insulation runs the full length of both copper lines up to the unit, no bare copper shows outside, and the hole where the pipes pass through the wall is sealed against rain.

  5. Outdoor unit and board

    The outdoor unit is level and firm, with no vibration carried into the wall, the breaker is labelled on the board, and there is an access hatch at every concealed unit.

Air conditioning maintenance: what does it really involve?

Most routine care is simple: clean filters, an outdoor unit rinsed of salt, a clear drain and a check before summer.

  • Filters: wash them regularly during the cooling season as the unit’s manual says, and more often in homes near the sea or with a lot of dust. A clogged filter weakens cooling and raises consumption.
  • Outdoor unit: rinse it with fresh water from time to time with the power off, without a high-pressure jet that bends the fins, and check the brackets for rust.
  • The drain: if the indoor unit drips, the line is blocked or its fall is wrong, and it needs clearing or correcting.
  • Before summer: a qualified technician checks the refrigerant pressure, the connections and the fan, and cleans the coils, before the rush for appointments.
  • Refrigerant is not used up: a unit that needs recharging has a leak, and the right fix is to find and repair the leak before recharging.
  • Switch the unit off and call a technician if ice forms on the pipes, it gets loud, the breaker trips, or there is a burning smell.

Our air conditioning work is the first fix, installation and commissioning, in a flat being finished or one already finished. If your system’s problem comes from the installation itself, such as a drain with no fall or an overloaded circuit, that is installation work: we survey it and write you a quotation.

Questions people ask

Where should the outdoor unit go in an Alexandria building?
Ask the building management first, because many buildings have rules on where outdoor units go: in the light well, on the roof or on a service balcony rather than the façade. Then choose a spot with enough air around it, brackets fixed into concrete with stainless bolts, and a drain that does not drip on the neighbours. Near the sea, add an anti-corrosion coated coil.
My air conditioner drips onto the neighbours’ balcony. What do I do?
Run the drain line to the balcony floor drain or to the drain stack with a trap, with a continuous fall all the way. If water comes from the indoor unit itself, the line is blocked, its fall is wrong, or it is not insulated and sweats. Where there is no natural fall, a small condensate pump can be fitted with the unit.
What size air conditioner do I need for a room?
There is no figure from floor area alone. What decides it: the room’s area and height, its orientation and the sun it gets, the glazing, whether the flat is under the roof, and how many people and appliances are in it. An oversized unit switches off before it removes the moisture, so the room ends up cold and clammy. We calculate the load for each room and tell you what we arrived at and why.
Split or concealed air conditioning for a flat?
A split is simpler and easier to service, and you can replace one unit without touching the rest, but you see it on the wall. A concealed ducted unit is invisible and quiet, but needs depth in the suspended ceiling and an access hatch at every unit, and has to be decided before the gypsum. You can mix them: concealed in the reception, splits in the bedrooms.
How often should air conditioning be serviced, and what does it involve?
Filters are washed regularly in the cooling season as the manual says, the outdoor unit is rinsed with fresh water to remove salt, and the drain line is checked. Before summer, a qualified technician checks the refrigerant and connections and cleans the coils. If the unit needs refrigerant every year, there is a leak to repair first. Our work is the first fix, supply and installation, and if the problem comes from the installation itself, we survey it and write you a quotation.
How much does air conditioning installation cost in Alexandria?
It depends on the number of units, their type and capacity, split or concealed, the length of the pipe runs, where the outdoor units go, whether the flat is being finished or already finished, and the state of the electrics. We do not price without seeing: we visit, measure and give you a written, itemised quotation; you pay suppliers directly at their price, and our management fee is agreed in writing.
