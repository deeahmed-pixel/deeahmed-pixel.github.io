Interior decoration in Alexandria: design and build

Decoration in Alexandria happens in older flats with details worth keeping and in newer flats close to the sea, and all of them live with humidity and salt air. Here is what separates decoration that lasts from decoration that tires quickly, how a project runs from survey to handover, and how to compare decoration firms before you choose.

Get a quotationWhatsApp

Design office or finishing company? The difference that matters

A design office draws and chooses; a finishing company builds. What matters to you is knowing who is responsible if what is built differs from what was drawn.

In the first route you hire a designer, then a contractor, and the drawings are the reference between them. That suits you if you have time to manage both, and if the drawings are detailed enough for more than one contractor to price them.

In the second route one company agrees the drawings and samples with you and builds them, so responsibility runs from drawing to installation, and the gap between the picture you liked and what gets built is smaller, provided the drawings and samples are approved in writing before work starts.

Itmam works both ways: we agree drawings and samples with you and build them, and if you already have a designer, we build to their drawings.

Decorating an older Alexandria flat: work with the building, not against it

An older flat in Alexandria often has things worth keeping, and good decoration builds on them instead of covering them up.

  • Tall ceilings in some older buildings are an asset: a gypsum board ceiling that hides the AC and lighting can cover part of the room or run round its edges, instead of lowering the whole ceiling.
  • Cornices, panelled doors, terrazzo floors and old marble can often be restored, so compare restoring with replacing before you decide.
  • Walls and floors in older buildings are rarely flat or square, so accurate measuring is essential before any cabinet or cladding is made, and pieces are adjusted on site.
  • Old wiring is tested before any concealed lighting or new appliances, because many Alexandria buildings have aluminium wiring and boards that have been extended again and again.
  • Old windows that have lost their seal let in salt air and dust, so decide whether to repair or replace them before painting and curtains.

If the flat needs a full renovation rather than decoration alone, our page on renovating older flats explains what changes and what is worth keeping.

Humidity and sea air: decoration materials that last in Alexandria

A material that looks lovely in a catalogue can struggle in a damp flat or one near the sea, so ask of every material: how does it behave with humidity and salt?

MaterialLikely problemWhat to do
WallpaperPeels, or mould grows behind it, if the wall is dampFix the cause of the damp first, choose a wipeable type, and avoid it on damp walls
Timber cladding and slatsWood moves with humidity, and ordinary board swellsMoisture-resistant boards, sealed edges and a ventilation gap behind the cladding
Gypsum boardStandard board suffers in bathrooms and kitchensMoisture-resistant boards in wet areas, and access panels at the AC units
PaintBlisters and flakes on a wall with seepage or saltsFix the cause, then a suitable filler and primer, and a washable paint
Marble and stoneCopes with humidity, but some stones absorb stainsThe right surface finish, and a sealer where the stone needs one
Metals and brassOrdinary steel rusts and brass darkens near the seaStainless steel suited to sea air, or powder-coated metal to a coastal specification, and regular cleaning

Before decorating a damp wall, find the cause: condensation, a leak or rising damp, because each is treated differently. Our guide to damp walls explains the difference.

What should be on paper before work starts?

The clearer the drawings and samples before work starts, the fewer changes during the job, and the easier it is to compare quotes and check the work at handover.

  • A furniture layout: where the sofas, bed, dining table and TV go, because they set the sockets and lighting.
  • A ceiling plan: the gypsum board levels and the positions of lights, AC and access panels.
  • An electrical plan: sockets, switches, TV and data points and concealed lighting, before the walls are closed.
  • Elevations of feature walls: the TV wall, cladding and built-in cabinets, with sizes and materials.
  • A finishes schedule: for each room the floor, walls, ceiling and paint, by product name or specification and colour.
  • Samples: stone from the showroom, paint samples applied to the flat’s own wall and seen in daylight and at night, and wood and fabric samples.

Pictures and 3D views help you imagine the result, but the dimensioned drawings and approved samples are what the work is built to and checked against.

How a decoration project runs, from survey to handover

  1. Survey and your brief

    We measure and photograph the flat, check damp, wiring and windows, and hear how you use the home and which pictures you like.

  2. Direction and samples

    We agree the direction, colours and materials, and you see the stone at our El Deresa showroom and the samples in person.

  3. Drawings and quotation

    You receive the drawings and a written, itemised quotation: gypsum, paint, cladding, fitted pieces, stone and electrics, each as its own item.

  4. Preparation

    Damp treatment, electrical and AC changes, and any necessary demolition, before the decorative work.

  5. Ceilings and walls

    Gypsum board and cladding go up, followed by filling and painting in stages.

  6. Workshop pieces and stone

    Doors, wardrobes, TV units, the kitchen and vanities are made in our workshops to the final measurements, and the stone is templated, cut and installed by the same team.

  7. Lighting and final finishes

    Light fittings, switches and accessories are fitted, then the last coat of paint goes on after the dusty work is finished.

  8. Handover

    You walk the flat with us room by room; snags are written down and closed before handover.

Working in an occupied Alexandria building

Most flat decoration in Alexandria happens in occupied buildings, so the building’s routine is part of the programme, not a side detail.

  • Working hours: ask the building management or the owners’ association which hours are allowed for noisy work such as demolition and cutting, and agree them in writing if you can.
  • The lift: find out whether it may be used for materials and debris, and measure it, because long boards and large pieces may have to go up the stairs.
  • Protecting the entrance and stairs: cover floors and walls along the route materials take, and clean them at the end of the day.
  • Debris: agree in advance where it is collected and how it is removed, and never leave it on the stairs or outside the building.
  • Loading: in narrow streets, ask where a vehicle can stop while loading and unloading.
  • Neighbours: any plumbing or bathroom work is tested before it is closed, so a problem never reaches the flat below.

Pieces made in the workshop arrive ready to fit, which means less cutting, dust and noise inside the building. We agree these points with you and the building management before we start.

How to compare decoration quotes

Compare what each quote includes before you compare the figures, because “decoration” can mean drawings only to one firm and full delivery with materials to another.

  • Design: are drawings included, and what exactly do they cover: layout, ceilings, electrics and elevations, or pictures only?
  • Items: is the quote broken down item by item with quantities and specifications, or one figure for the whole flat?
  • Materials: is every material named by product or specification, thickness and colour? Who buys it, and is the invoice in your name?
  • Fitted pieces: doors, wardrobes and TV units, in which board, with which finish and which hardware?
  • What is excluded: loose furniture, curtains, light fittings and appliances are often outside the quote, so ask for that in writing.
  • Payment: ideally in stages tied to work that is actually finished and accepted.
  • Changes: how is a change during the job priced and approved? Ask for it in writing.

Warning signs: a quote without a survey, a single figure with no items, or pressure to pay a large sum before any drawings exist.

Questions people ask

What is the difference between a design office and a finishing company?
A design office draws and chooses materials; a finishing company builds. If you use two firms, the drawings are the reference between them and must be detailed. If one firm does both, responsibility runs from drawing to installation, provided the drawings and samples are approved in writing before work starts.
Do you design, or only build?
Both. We agree drawings and samples with you before anything is made, and if you already have a designer, we build to their drawings. Either way, the written quotation shows every item separately.
Can I redecorate an older flat without much demolition?
Usually, yes, if the wiring, pipes and walls are in good condition. Paint, gypsum board over part of the ceiling, a clad feature wall and made-to-measure pieces can change a flat without heavy demolition. Test the wiring first, though, and treat any damp before painting or papering.
Who is the best decoration firm in Alexandria?
No single firm is best for everyone. The best one for you surveys before pricing, gives you drawings and a detailed quotation with materials named, shows you real work, and accepts payment in stages tied to finished work. Compare quotes item by item, and ask who buys the materials and whose name is on the invoice.
Do you decorate offices and clinics in Alexandria?
Yes, we have finished offices and clinics. The layout, lighting, floors and reception are planned around how you work and around the building itself, and building management sometimes asks for drawings before work starts, so ask them early.
How much does decorating a flat in Alexandria cost?
It depends on the area, the state of the flat, and the items involved: ceilings, lighting, paint, cladding, fitted pieces and stone, and the materials you choose. We do not price without a survey: we measure and give you a written, itemised quotation; you pay suppliers directly at their price, and our management fee is agreed in writing.
