Blocks & Bricks

Blocks and bricks go out by the pallet, the thousand or the lorry load to whoever is building, with no requirement that we lay a single one. Owner-builders, small contractors and consultants ordering for a client all buy on the same trade footing, delivered to site across Cairo, the coast and everywhere between. We quote against the count and the delivery on the day you ask, because the walling market moves with fuel and fired-clay costs and a published figure would only mislead you.

Get a quote for blocks & bricks
  1. 01

    Red clay brick

    The familiar fired brick at around 25 by 12 by 6 centimetres, solid and perforated. Solid where you want mass, sound resistance and a wall that will take a heavy fixing without a resin anchor. Perforated where you want the same wall lighter and a little more thermally forgiving.

  2. 02

    Cement blocks, hollow & solid

    The standard Egyptian face of 40 by 20 centimetres in 10, 12, 15 and 20 centimetre thicknesses, hollow and solid. Fast to lay, dimensionally consistent and the default for external skins, party walls and any partition that has to carry something.

  3. 03

    Lightweight & autoclaved aerated

    Aerated blocks at roughly a third the weight of solid concrete for the same volume, in large formats that go up quickly. Genuinely good insulation, easy to cut with a hand saw, and kind to a slab you are trying not to overload. Laid on thin-bed adhesive mortar, not a thick sand-cement bed, which is the mistake that eats the saving.

  4. 04

    Hourdi & hollow floor units

    Infill blocks for ribbed and hollow-block slabs, laid between the ribs to form the soffit and take the topping. Supplied in the depths that suit the rib spacing on your drawing. Order the depth against the structural detail, not against what the last job used.

  5. 05

    Thermal & insulated blocks

    Blocks with an insulating core or a lightweight aggregate, for external walls where the cooling load is the thing you are actually fighting. Worth costing properly on a west-facing Sahel elevation or a New Cairo villa with a lot of exposed wall, where the saving is on the air conditioning bill rather than on the wall.

Selection

Walling units and where they belong

  • Solid red clay brickMass, sound & wet areas

    Heavy, dense and dependable. Best where you want acoustic separation, a wall that takes fixings anywhere, or masonry that will sit against water without complaint. The cost is dead weight the structure has to carry.

  • Hollow cement blockGeneral walling

    The everyday external skin and partition unit. Quick to lay, straight, and roughly forty per cent lighter than the solid version of the same size. Specify this unless something on the drawing genuinely needs solid.

  • Solid cement blockLoaded & wet walls

    For low retaining, plant rooms, wet areas, parapets that take wind load and any wall carrying a real fixing. Do not use it as a blanket default — it is nearly double the weight of hollow, and that weight is paid for twice.

  • Autoclaved aerated & lightweightPartitions & thermal skins

    The lightest option and the best insulator of the group. Ideal on upper floors, roof-level rooms and additions on an existing slab. Weak in tension, so it wants proper lintels, and fixings need the right plugs rather than optimism.

  • Hourdi & floor infillRibbed slabs

    Not a wall unit at all — the infill that forms a ribbed slab between the ribs. Ordered to the depth in the structural drawing, delivered to floor level where the crane or hoist can reach it.

  • Thermal & insulated blocksExternal envelope

    Insulating core or lightweight aggregate, for elevations taking real sun. The case for them is the running cost of cooling the rooms behind, which is where they pay back, not the wall cost.

Questions we get asked

Blocks & Bricks — questions we get asked

Will you supply blocks if somebody else is laying them?
Yes, and that is most of what goes out. Supply only, no contract, no minimum. Your own blockwork gang or your own contractor lays them, we deliver to site, and you buy at the same trade terms we buy at for our own jobs. If you want the delivery split across several drops as the floors go up, say so at the point of ordering and we will schedule it.
Hollow or solid — does it really matter?
It matters more than most owners think, and it is a common way to quietly overspend. A solid 20 centimetre block runs near 30 kilograms against roughly 18 for the hollow equivalent, so a solid wall lands somewhere near 400 kilograms on every square metre against nearer 250 for hollow. That extra dead load runs down through the beams, the columns and the footings, and it is paid for in concrete and reinforcement long before anybody sees the wall. Specify solid where you genuinely need it — wet areas, low retaining, parapets, walls carrying heavy fixings — and hollow everywhere else.
Are lightweight and AAC blocks worth it in Egypt?
On upper floors, roof rooms, additions over an existing slab and hot external elevations, frequently yes. They weigh roughly a third of solid concrete, insulate far better, and cut quickly with a hand saw so waste and labour both drop. Two conditions. They must be laid in thin-bed adhesive mortar rather than a thick sand-cement bed, or the thermal benefit and the speed both disappear. And fixings need the correct plugs, because a standard plug in aerated block will pull straight out under a wall-hung basin or a heavy cabinet.
What are hourdi blocks for?
They are floor units, not walling. In a ribbed slab the blocks sit between the concrete ribs, form the flat soffit you plaster later, and stay in place permanently — they carry very little load themselves. The depth has to match the rib spacing and slab depth on the structural drawing. Ordering the wrong depth is not something you can adapt around on site, so send us the detail or the depth from the drawing when you order.
How many blocks do I need per square metre?
For the standard 40 by 20 centimetre face, work on about 12 and a half blocks per square metre of wall before openings, plus around 5 per cent for cuts and breakage. For the 25 by 12 by 6 centimetre brick, a half-brick wall is nearer 55 to 60 per square metre. Take the gross wall area, deduct the openings, add the wastage. Send us the elevations and we will do the take-off with you rather than have you order twice.
