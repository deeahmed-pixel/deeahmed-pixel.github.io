Painting & Decorative Finishes

Paint is the finish everybody judges and the trade everybody rushes. Most of a good wall happens before any colour appears: filling, sanding, stabilising, priming and checking the surface with a lamp held flat against it. We work to a system — substrate, putty, primer, then finish coats — and where a wall is meant to be an event rather than a background, it goes to the decorative crew for Venetian plaster, micro-cement, metallic glaze or a painted mural.

What we actually do.

Get a quote for painting & decorative finishes
  1. 01

    Preparation & substrate

    The part everyone skips. Old coatings tested for adhesion, chalky and flaking paint stripped back, cracks cut out and filled, damp traced to its source rather than sealed over, and every surface stabilised before a primer is opened.

  2. 02

    Putty, skim & priming

    Two to three coats of putty, sanded flat and checked under raking light, then a primer chosen for what is underneath — alkali-resistant on fresh plaster, bonding primer on glossy or previously enamelled surfaces.

  3. 03

    Emulsion & enamel systems

    Matt, silk and scrubbable emulsions on walls and ceilings, and acrylic or polyurethane enamel on doors, frames and joinery, sprayed or laid off by hand with the substrate sanded between coats.

  4. 04

    Decorative finishes & murals

    Venetian plaster and stucco burnished to a polish, micro-cement on walls and floors, textured and metallic finishes, and hand-painted murals or feature panels drawn to scale before anyone touches the wall.

  5. 05

    Exterior & coastal coatings

    Render repair, alkali-resistant primer and elastomeric or silicone-based topcoats on external walls, specified for UV, wind-driven rain and the salt load that strips ordinary emulsion off a Sahel façade in two seasons.

Selection

Finishes we apply

  • Standard emulsion packageWhole house

    Prepared, puttied, primed and finished properly. Unremarkable when it is right, and the single most visible failure in a house when it is not.

  • Venetian plaster & stuccoFeature walls

    Lime-based plaster applied in thin layers and burnished to depth and sheen. It reads as stone rather than as paint, and it cannot be rushed.

  • Micro-cementWalls & floors

    A seamless mineral coating over walls, floors and even wet areas, with no joints. Wants a stable, crack-free substrate and a sealer suited to the room.

  • Metallic & texturedAccent surfaces

    Pearl, bronze and copper glazes, sand and travertine textures, and roller or trowel patterns for a wall that changes with the light through the day.

  • Murals & feature wallsBedrooms & halls

    Botanical, geometric or figurative panels drawn to scale and painted on site, sized to the wall and to the furniture that will sit in front of it.

  • Exterior coastal coatingNorth Coast

    Render repair and a flexible topcoat system for chalets and villas from Marina to New Alamein, facing salt, sun and wind — the exposure that turns a cheap façade paint chalky in a single season.

Frequently asked

Painting & Decorative Finishes — questions we get asked

Why does new paint peel or crack within a year?
Because the wall under it was never ready. Fresh plaster painted before it dried, putty applied over a dusty surface, no primer between putty and emulsion, glossy old enamel painted over without a bonding coat, or damp that was covered rather than traced. Paint is a thin film — it faithfully reports whatever is underneath it, usually in the first winter.
How many coats is actually a proper job?
Primer plus two finish coats as a minimum, and three on deep reds, blues and greys that never cover in two. Two coats straight onto bare putty is not a system, it is a cost saving that shows as patchiness the moment sunlight crosses the wall at an angle. We quote the system, not a number of buckets.
What is micro-cement and where does it actually work?
A thin mineral coating, a few millimetres thick, applied in layers over a prepared substrate and sealed. It gives a seamless surface on walls, floors and even shower areas with no grout lines, which is why people want it. It needs a stable substrate — it will telegraph any crack that moves underneath it — and the sealer must match the use, matt and hard-wearing on a floor, water-resistant in a bathroom.
How long should new plaster dry before painting?
Longer than your programme would like, and the number depends on thickness, weather and ventilation rather than on a rule. We check moisture rather than count days, prime with an alkali-resistant primer because fresh plaster is chemically aggressive, and if the schedule genuinely cannot wait we will tell you which system tolerates it and what the trade-off is.
Why does exterior paint fail so fast in the North Coast?
Salt, ultraviolet and wind-driven rain together, on a façade that also expands and contracts every day. Ordinary interior-grade emulsion pushed outside goes chalky in a season and then lets water into the render. We repair the render first, prime it, and use a flexible silicone-based or elastomeric topcoat that bridges hairline movement — then recommend a plain fresh-water wash each year, which does more than most owners expect.
