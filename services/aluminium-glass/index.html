Aluminium & Glass Works

Glass is unforgiving. It is either measured right or it is scrap, because nothing can be trimmed once it is toughened. Our glazing crew templates on site, processes off site and comes back to fit — frameless shower enclosures, balustrades, sliding walls, printed and sandblasted panels, mirrors and thermal-break aluminium — with fixings specified for where the house actually stands. Within sight of the sea that means seaside-class coating and stainless, not whatever arrived on the pallet.

Scope of works

What we actually do.

Everything below happens inside our business — no subcontracted links in the chain.

Get a quote for aluminium & glass works
  1. 01

    Shower enclosures

    Frameless and framed enclosures in 8 and 10 mm toughened glass, walk-in panels and wet room screens, with hinges, channels and handles in brushed steel, matt black, chrome or PVD gold, and a factory-applied coating that keeps limescale off the glass.

  2. 02

    Balustrades & stair screens

    Toughened laminated balustrades on stairs, landings, terraces and pool edges, in a base channel or on stainless point fixings, with or without a handrail — calculated for the load rather than copied from a photograph.

  3. 03

    Sliding, folding & pivot glass

    Sliding and lift-and-slide doors, folding walls that open a reception onto a terrace, and oversized pivot entrance doors, in minimal-frame or thermal-break systems with tracks set into the floor build-up.

  4. 04

    Decorative glass & mirrors

    Sandblasted, acid-etched, digitally printed, back-painted and antiqued glass for doors, screens and splashbacks, plus mirrored walls, bronze and grey mirror and mirror inset into wardrobe and joinery fronts.

  5. 05

    Aluminium systems & curtain walling

    Thermal-break windows and doors, curtain walling and glazed screens, with seaside-class powder coating, drained and vented frames, and hardware chosen to survive a coastal winter of standing salt.

    Full-height mirror walls, bronze and smoked mirror panels, and mirror inset into wardrobe fronts, cut and polished to fit the joinery rather than the other way round.

Questions we get asked

Aluminium & Glass Works — questions we get asked

Frameless or framed shower enclosure?
Frameless looks better and shows the tiling or stone behind it, and it costs more because the glass is thicker, the hardware is structural and the tolerances are unforgiving — the walls have to be plumb, and on a renovation they often are not. Framed enclosures are cheaper, seal more easily on an out-of-square opening and are simpler to adjust later. We measure your actual walls before recommending one.
Is a glass balustrade safe on a stair or a terrace?
When it is laminated, yes. Laminated glass holds together on the interlayer if a pane is broken, which is why we use it on balustrades rather than plain toughened, and the fixings — base channel or point fixings — are sized for a horizontal load, not chosen for looks. On a terrace we also specify stainless of the right grade, because the fixing corrodes long before the glass does.
Why does shower glass go cloudy after a year?
That is limescale from hard water etching into the surface, not the glass failing. A factory-applied protective coating slows it dramatically, and a squeegee after each use does more than any product sold for the purpose. What ruins shower glass and hardware fastest is acidic descaler left to sit on it — it will pit chrome and gold plating permanently.
What changes for glass and aluminium near the sea?
The metal, not the glass. Standard powder coating and standard fixings will bloom and stain within a couple of seasons on a seafront chalet, so we specify seaside-class coating, A4 stainless fittings, and frames that drain and vent properly so salt water is not sitting in the profile all winter. Then a fresh-water rinse a few times a year keeps it looking new.
How is this different from your windows and doors page?
Windows and doors covers complete window systems for the house — thermal break, uPVC, double glazing, the whole opening. This page is the glass trade around it: shower enclosures, balustrades, screens, decorative and printed glass, mirrors and the aluminium and curtain walling that carries them. On most projects the same drawings feed both, and you deal with one team.
