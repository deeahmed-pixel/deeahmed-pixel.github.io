Build & MEP

Stables & Equestrian Facilities

Horses break buildings designed for people. We build yards the way a working yard needs them — loose boxes with drainage falls and anti-cast rails, an American barn with a through-draught, a wash bay that actually drains, a tack room that stays dry, and an arena whose footing is a drainage system with sand on top rather than a sandpit with a fence round it.

Scope of works

What we actually do.

Everything below happens inside our business — no subcontracted links in the chain.

Get a quote for stables & equestrian
  1. 01

    Loose boxes & American barns

    Individual stables at 3.65 x 3.65 m as a working minimum and 4.3 x 4.3 m for larger horses and foaling, with kick-boarded lower walls, anti-cast rails, galvanised grilles and yokes, safe glazing, top-hung sliding or split doors, and ridge and soffit ventilation. American barns with a central aisle wide enough to turn a horse and cross-tie it.

  2. 02

    Outdoor arenas & manages

    A 20 x 40 m or 20 x 60 m arena built as four layers: a compacted stone base laid to fall, perimeter drainage, a geotextile membrane, and a silica sand and fibre topping at a consistent depth. Kicking boards, gates and mirrors as specified. Get the base wrong and no amount of sand fixes it.

  3. 03

    Horse walkers & lunging rings

    Bases and structures for four to six bay mechanical horse walkers — the circular exerciser — with the same drainage build-up as the arena, panelled or electrified partitions and a roof against the sun. Lunging rings and round pens at 15 to 20 m diameter with a kicking board and even footing to the edge.

  4. 04

    Wash bays, solariums, tack & feed stores

    Wash bays with non-slip falls to a silt trap, hot and cold supply and a hose boom; horse solariums with the correct height and electrical protection over a wet floor; tack rooms with humidity control, saddle racks and security; and vermin-proof feed and hay stores kept away from the boxes for fire separation.

  5. 05

    Paddocks, turnout & yard infrastructure

    Post-and-rail fencing with hot wire, gates a horse cannot lift, automatic water troughs on frost and dust protection, all-weather turnout pens, muck store, hay barn, lorry hardstanding and the yard drainage that connects the whole lot.

  • Private stable blockVillas & farms

    Two to eight boxes with a feed store, tack room and wash bay, built for an owner who keeps horses at home rather than at livery. Common on the larger plots around Sheikh Zayed and the Sakkara road.

  • American barnStuds

    Boxes either side of a covered central aisle, with cross-ties, a wash bay in the run and stores at one end. Better in wind and dust, and far better to work in through a Cairo summer.

  • Outdoor arena / sand schoolManage

    Base, drainage, membrane and a sand-and-fibre surface built to a specification you can ride on in February. Kicking boards, gates, mirrors and lighting to suit the discipline.

  • Horse walker & lunging ringExercise

    A level, drained, roofed base for a mechanical walker, and a round pen with consistent footing right out to the kicking board so the horse is not working on a slope at the edge.

  • Wash bay & solariumCare

    Non-slip falls, silt trap, hot water and a solarium hung and protected correctly over a wet floor. Small structures that get built badly more often than anything else on a yard.

  • Paddocks & turnoutLand

    Fencing, gates, water, shade shelters and all-weather turnout pens, laid out so horses can be moved without opening the whole yard.

Questions we get asked

Stables & Equestrian — questions we get asked

How big should a loose box be?
For an average riding horse, 3.65 x 3.65 m is the working minimum and anything smaller is a stall rather than a box. Larger horses, stallions and foaling boxes want 4.3 x 4.3 m or more. Height matters as much as floor area — a low roof over a box in Egypt turns into an oven by midday, so we build tall, vent at the ridge and let air in at the soffit.
What actually goes into an arena surface?
Four layers, and the sand is the least important of them. A compacted stone base laid to fall, perimeter drainage, a geotextile membrane to stop the sand migrating into the stone, then 10 to 12 cm of washed silica sand blended with fibre. Silica because it stays angular and holds its shape; fibre because it stops the sand shifting under a hoof. Building sand and dune sand both ride badly and neither drains.
Why does my sand school go deep in places and set hard in others?
Almost always inconsistent depth over a base that was never laid to a proper fall, so water sits in low spots and the surface migrates towards the track. We survey the existing arena, correct the base and drainage, replace or amend the topping, and set depth markers at the boards so it can be maintained to a number instead of by eye.
Do you build horse walkers and lunging rings?
We build everything the walker sits on and everything around it — the level drained base, the perimeter, the partitions, the roof and the power and control supply — and we install the manufacturer equipment. A walker on a poorly drained or out-of-level base wears its bearings and its horses unevenly, which is why the groundwork is the part worth paying attention to.
Do you work outside Cairo?
Yes. The yards around Sakkara and Abu Sir are routine for us, as are the larger plots at Sheikh Zayed and the North Coast, and we take on stud work anywhere in the country. Materials for arenas are the long lead item — washed silica sand and fibre have to be ordered and tested, not bought off the back of a lorry.
