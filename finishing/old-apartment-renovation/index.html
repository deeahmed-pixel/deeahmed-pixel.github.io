Renovating older apartments

Older apartments in Alexandria have generous rooms, high ceilings and details that are hard to find today, and also wiring, pipes and waterproofing that have had their time. A good renovation starts with what is behind the wall before what is on it.

Get a quotationWhatsApp

What goes wrong in older apartments

The real problems in an old flat are usually hidden behind the finish: the wiring, the pipes and the waterproofing.

  • Aluminium or aged wiring, a board that has been extended again and again, and no earth-leakage protection.
  • Galvanised steel water pipes that have corroded inside, so the pressure is weak and the water runs discoloured.
  • Bathrooms whose waterproofing has failed and that leak onto the flat below.
  • Damp in the walls, especially on ground floors and on walls that carry pipes.
  • Uneven floors and loose tiles that sound hollow.
  • Old timber or aluminium windows that let in air, dust and noise.

The survey and the written quotation

A renovation starts with a survey that looks behind the finish, not with a colour chart. It should cover:

  • Testing the electrics, the board and the cables, and finding out what type they are and their condition.
  • Inspecting the supply pipes, the drainage and the water pressure.
  • Finding the source of any damp or leak before any treatment.
  • Checking floors, ceilings and windows.
  • Listing the details worth keeping.

The result goes into the quotation item by item: what must be replaced, what can be repaired, and what stays as it is.

Full or partial renovation

Not every old flat needs stripping back to the walls; the decision depends on the state of the wiring, plumbing and waterproofing, not on how dated the tiles look.

ConditionUsually the right approach
Old or aluminium wiring, corroded pipes, leaking bathroomsRenew all the services, which means cutting into walls and floors
Services already renewed and in good orderRenew the finishes: floors, paint, kitchen and bathrooms
Bathroom waterproofing and pipes sound, but the look is datedReplace sanitary ware and fittings, or apply a new surface over the old tiles if they are firmly bonded
Walls sound, paint tiredTreat cracks and damp, then repaint, with no demolition

A new surface over old tiles, such as microcement, only works if the tiles beneath are firmly fixed and the waterproofing is sound; otherwise the problem stays hidden under the new look.

Wiring and plumbing: renew or not

If the wiring is aluminium or has no earth-leakage protection, or the pipes are old galvanised steel, renewing them is the safe choice and the one that pays back over time.

  • Copper cables sized for today's air conditioners, water heaters and appliances.
  • A new board with separate circuits and an earth-leakage breaker.
  • PPR supply pipes in place of galvanised steel, and PVC drainage laid to correct falls.
  • A check of the flat's connection to the building's main riser, because renovating the flat will not fix a problem in the riser itself.

What is worth keeping

Old apartments have details that add value, and their fate is best decided before demolition, not after.

  • High ceilings: you can drop gypsum board only where it is needed and keep the full height elsewhere.
  • Old plaster cornices and mouldings: some can be repaired rather than removed.
  • Solid old timber doors: they can often be stripped, refinished and given new hardware.
  • Mosaic (terrazzo) floor tiles: if they are firm and do not sound hollow, they can often be ground and polished instead of lifted.
  • Old marble: it can often be ground and polished if its thickness and condition allow.

The decision is made after the survey: sometimes restoring is better, sometimes replacing suits the material's condition.

Damp: find the cause first

Treating damp starts with finding where it comes from, because paint over a wet wall will peel again.

  • A leak from pipes or a bathroom, in the flat itself or in the flat above.
  • Rising damp from the ground on ground floors.
  • Rainwater coming through the façade or around the windows.
  • Condensation in bathrooms and kitchens without enough ventilation.
  • Salts appearing on the wall surface (efflorescence), which often means the affected plaster has to be removed and the wall treated before re-plastering.

Once the source is stopped, the wall needs time to dry, then treatment, plaster and a suitable paint. If the source is in a neighbour's flat or the building's riser, the fix needs their cooperation.

Working in an occupied building

Renovating in an old, occupied building needs organisation that respects the neighbours; otherwise the work gets stopped.

  • Working hours agreed with the building, especially for demolition and noise.
  • Protection for the stairs, entrance and lift while materials and debris move.
  • Debris removed as it is produced, never left on the stairs or in front of the building.
  • Neighbours told in advance before water or power to the riser is shut off.
  • If you stay in the flat, the work is split into phases, room by room.

Questions people ask

Where do I start renovating an old flat?
With a survey of the wiring, plumbing and damp before choosing any finish. Once you know what is behind the walls, you can decide between a full and a partial renovation and order the work: services first, then waterproofing, then finishes.
Do I have to rewire everything?
Not always, but if the wiring is aluminium or aged and there is no earth-leakage protection, a full rewire is the safe decision. If it was already renewed in copper and is in good condition, a new board and extra circuits for air conditioning may be enough. Testing decides this, not appearance.
Can a bathroom be renovated without demolition?
Yes, if the waterproofing and pipes are sound: you can change the sanitary ware and fittings, or apply a new surface over firmly bonded tiles. If the bathroom leaks onto the flat below or the pipes are corroded, demolition is the honest answer, because a new surface will not stop a leak.
Can we live there during the works?
Often yes for a partial renovation, with the work split into phases. For a full rewire and replumb it is hard, because water and power will be off at times and dust gets everywhere. Many owners move out for the heaviest stage and come back for the finishes.
Can the old mosaic (terrazzo) floor be restored?
Often yes, if the tiles are firmly bonded and do not sound hollow: grinding and polishing can bring back the surface. Loose or broken tiles have to be replaced, and an exact colour match can be hard to find. The decision is made at the survey.
