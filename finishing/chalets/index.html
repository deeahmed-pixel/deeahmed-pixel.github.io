Chalet finishing by the sea

A chalet is lived in for a few weeks a year and shut for the rest, next to a sea that brings salt, damp and sand. Finishing it well means materials that can take that, and a plan that respects the season and your compound's rules.

Get a quotationWhatsApp

A chalet is not a small apartment

A chalet is designed for a different kind of use: a short, crowded season of wet feet and sand, then months with the door locked. That changes what a good finish looks like:

  • Sand comes in with every step, so floors must resist abrasion and clean easily.
  • In season there are more people than bedrooms, so storage and extra sleeping places matter.
  • Bathrooms work far harder over the summer than in an ordinary flat, so waterproofing and drainage must be right.
  • If the chalet will be rented out, finishes should be easy to maintain and to replace.
  • The terrace or garden is usually where the family actually spends the day.

Materials that survive salt and humidity

By the sea, ordinary metals rust and ordinary boards swell, so every material has to be chosen for the place, not for the photograph.

Screws, hinges and fittingsPlain steel and low-grade stainless rust and stain316 stainless and marine-grade hardware
Steel railings, gates, pergolasSteel that is only painted, with no galvanisingHot-dip galvanised steel, then a suitable paint system
Aluminium windows and shuttersStandard powder coating that chalks and peelsSeaside-class powder coating
Kitchen and wardrobe carcassesStandard boards that absorb moisture and swellMoisture-resistant boards with well-sealed edges
Outdoor worktopsQuartz, which can discolour in direct sunGranite or natural stone

Floors, bathrooms and kitchen for summer use

The best chalet floor is one that takes sand and water without becoming slippery or showing every scratch.

  • Matt or lightly textured porcelain: hard, non-absorbent and easy to clean of sand.
  • Marble is beautiful but softer, and sand scratches it over time, especially at the entrance and the terrace door. If you choose it, a honed finish wears better than a polished one.
  • Solid timber and parquet are not the best choice with sand and humidity.
  • Bathrooms: correct falls to the floor drain, full waterproofing under the tiles, and an outdoor shower by the terrace to keep sand out of the house.
  • Kitchen: moisture-resistant carcasses, stainless hinges and runners, and a granite or quartz top depending on how much sun reaches it.

Small spaces: storage and extra beds

In a small chalet every centimetre has to work, and the answer is usually fitted joinery rather than more furniture.

  • Wardrobes to the ceiling, with the high shelves for seasonal things.
  • Beds with drawers underneath, or a sofa bed in the living room.
  • A place for the washing machine, the vacuum cleaner and beach gear, away from the kitchen if possible.
  • Storage for beach chairs and umbrellas close to the terrace.
  • Lighting on separate circuits: general light, and softer light for the evening.

Terrace and garden

A chalet terrace works as an extra room, so it needs waterproofing, falls, shade and light like one.

  • Waterproofing under the terrace floor if there is a room or another unit below it, and falls towards the drains.
  • Slip-resistant outdoor paving that stays relatively cool underfoot: textured natural stone or outdoor-grade porcelain.
  • A pergola in timber, galvanised steel or aluminium; each needs different upkeep by the sea.
  • Outdoor light fittings made for humidity, and covered sockets protected by an earth-leakage breaker.
  • For a ground-floor chalet with a garden, plan for the sand and irrigation water that can reach the door: a proper threshold and a drain in front of the terrace door.

Protecting a chalet that sits closed all winter

A chalet left shut for months needs provisions that protect it with nobody there.

  • A main water valve that is easy to reach and closed before you leave, or a valve that shuts the water automatically when a leak is detected.
  • Surge protection on the electrical board for anything left running.
  • Shutters that close well, against sun, sand and intruders.
  • Simple ventilation in bathrooms and wardrobes to stop mould and the smell of damp.
  • Good sealing around windows and doors against sand and rain.

Timing, and your compound's rules

The best time to finish a chalet is just after the season, so that there is time for decisions and for making the fitted items before the next summer.

Each North Coast compound sets its own rules: whether works are allowed in season, working hours, how contractors register, how debris leaves the site, and which changes are allowed to the façade and terrace. Get the rules in writing from the compound management before you fix any dates, rather than relying on what someone remembers.

Made-to-order items such as the kitchen, shutters and aluminium need final measurements, so the earlier the decisions are settled, the more comfortable the schedule.

Questions people ask

Can you finish my chalet before summer?
That depends on when the work starts and how much there is to do, so the honest answer comes after the survey, not before it. The earlier you start after the season and settle the kitchen, shutters and floors, the more time there is to make and install them. Your compound's rules on working periods also shape the schedule.
What flooring is best for a chalet?
Matt or lightly textured porcelain is the easiest choice: hard, non-absorbent and simple to clean of sand. Marble works if you accept it will scratch over time, preferably in a honed finish. Outside, choose stone or outdoor porcelain that is not slippery when wet.
Aluminium and steel corrode by the sea. What's the fix?
The fix is in the specification from day one: aluminium with seaside-class powder coating, steel hot-dip galvanised before painting, and 316 stainless screws and fittings. After installation, rinsing metal surfaces with fresh water from time to time removes the salt that builds up.
Do you furnish chalets too?
We make the fitted pieces in our own workshops: the kitchen, wardrobes, doors and bathroom vanities. For loose furniture such as sofas and beds, tell us exactly what you need, and the quotation will make clear what we can provide.
How do I protect a chalet that sits closed all winter?
Close the main water valve before you leave, keep surge protection on the board for anything left on, and shut the shutters properly. If you are finishing it now, ask for a valve that closes automatically on a leak, ventilation for bathrooms and wardrobes, and good sealing around windows against sand.
