Roof and bathroom waterproofing

Waterproofing is a layer nobody sees once the finishing is done, which is why its mistakes show late: a stain on the neighbour’s ceiling, peeling paint, or tiles that have to come up. Here is why leaks happen, the types of waterproofing, and the right order before the tiles.

Why leaks happen

Most leaks do not come through the middle of the waterproofing layer but through the details: corners, drains and pipes, cracks, and places where water stands.

  • Floor-to-wall corners: if the waterproofing is not turned up the wall far enough, water runs under the tiles into the wall and then down.
  • Around drains and pipes coming through the floor: the weakest point of any system, needing reinforcement pieces and a carefully welded or bonded joint.
  • Wrong falls: water that stands on a roof eventually finds any crack.
  • Cracks in the concrete and plaster: if they are not treated before waterproofing, they move and split a rigid layer.
  • Damage after waterproofing: an unprotected layer punctured by a nail or a tool while the tiles or a pergola go in.
  • The pipes themselves: sometimes the stain on a ceiling is not the waterproofing at all, but a leaking pipe or joint inside the wall or floor.

Types of waterproofing: what’s the difference?

No single system is best everywhere; the choice depends on the place, whether it will be tiled over or left exposed to the sun, and whether the surface moves or cracks. In general terms, these are the common systems:

SystemSuitsWatch for
Torch-applied bitumen membranesConcrete roofs, under tiles, and buried structure.The laps between rolls and how they are welded, and turning them up the parapets; they usually need a protection layer against sun and knocks.
Cementitious coatings, rigid or flexible, brushed onBathrooms, kitchens, water tanks and basements.Rigid coatings cannot take crack movement, so flexible ones suit where there is slight movement; they go on a clean, damp surface in at least two coats.
Liquid-applied membranes that cure to a seamless layerRoofs, terraces and complicated details around pipes.The thickness must be even everywhere, and some types can be left exposed to the sun while others must be covered.
Bituminous coatingsFoundations and buried walls below ground.Most are not made to stay exposed to the sun on a roof.

More important than the product name are three things: preparing the surface before it, the details at corners and drains, and the flood test after it.

Roof waterproofing: water and heat

Proper roof waterproofing is a set of layers in a fixed order, and each layer is checked before the next one covers it.

  1. Inspect and clean

    Remove old failed layers and debris, inspect the concrete slab and treat its cracks.

  2. Falls

    A layer laid to falls towards the drains, so water does not stand anywhere on the roof.

  3. Prepare corners and drains

    A fillet or chamfer in the corner between the roof and the parapet, so the waterproofing does not crack over a sharp angle, and the drains and rainwater pipes prepared.

  4. Waterproofing layer

    One continuous layer over the whole roof, turned up the parapets and protected at the top, and sealed around every pipe and base.

  5. Flood test

    The drains are blocked and the roof is flooded for 24 to 48 hours, and the top-floor ceiling is checked from below.

  6. Insulation and protection

    Insulation boards and a protection layer, then the final finish. The order of insulation and waterproofing differs between systems; what matters is that the waterproofing is never left open to knocks or sun unless it is made for that.

Heat insulation cuts the heat reaching the top floor in summer, and a light-coloured roof finish helps too. The roof finish itself — floors and pergolas — is covered on the roof finishing page.

Bathrooms and kitchens

Bathroom waterproofing covers the whole floor and turns up the walls, not only the shower area, because water travels anywhere under tiles.

  • Waterproofing across the whole bathroom floor, turned up the walls to a sensible height, and over almost the full wall in the shower zone.
  • A reinforcing tape or piece in the corners, around the drain and around every pipe coming through the floor or wall.
  • The drain fitted so the waterproofing bonds into it tightly, rather than simply stopping around it.
  • Falls under the tiles towards the drain, so water does not stand in a corner of the shower.
  • In the kitchen: waterproofing under the sink area, at the washing machine position and around any floor drain.
  • Plumbing first fix pressure-tested before waterproofing, because a leaking pipe under sound waterproofing will still soak the wall and floor.

The flood test, before the tiles

A flood test means blocking the drain, filling the bathroom or roof with water a few centimetres deep and leaving it for 24 to 48 hours, then checking the ceiling below and the walls around it for any damp.

Why before the tiles? Because repairing waterproofing while it is exposed means patching a small area; after tiling, it means lifting tiles and bedding, and sometimes the sanitary ware, to reach the leak.

Ask for the test to be recorded with photos and a date, and keep the record with your handover papers. After the test, protect the waterproofing until the tiles go down, so nobody punctures it during the work.

Old buildings and basements

In older buildings, the stain on your ceiling often comes from the bathroom or kitchen of the flat above, or from shared pipes, so find the source before any painting, or the stain will come back. Sometimes the cause is rising damp or condensation, and each needs a different treatment; the damp walls guide covers them.

In basements, the best waterproofing is on the outside, against the soil, while the building goes up. In an existing basement there are cementitious systems applied from the inside that resist water pressure from behind, together with stopping any active leak and draining water away from the building where possible.

When you renovate a bathroom in an old flat, lift the old tiles and expose the pipes, then waterproof and test again, rather than patching over the old.

Itmam and waterproofing

For us, waterproofing is part of bathroom, roof and terrace work, not a separate item rushed through. We visit, try to trace the source of the leak, and give you a written, itemised quotation before work starts, with the system, the areas, the upturns and the flood test as clear items.

The flood test is recorded with photos before any tile goes down. If you are doing the work yourself or with your own contractor, waterproofing materials are available supply-only — cementitious coatings, bitumen membranes and coatings, and liquid-applied systems — and we will tell you which one suits your problem.

On a finishing contract you pay each supplier directly and the tax invoice is in your name; our fee for supervision and execution is agreed in writing before we begin. We work in Alexandria, on the North Coast and in New Cairo, and elsewhere in Egypt when the job suits us.

Questions people ask

What’s the best way to waterproof a roof?
No one product is best everywhere. The right method is to clean the roof and treat the cracks, lay falls to the drains, apply one continuous layer turned up the parapets, flood test it, then add insulation and a protection layer. The product depends on the roof’s condition and whether it will be tiled or left exposed.
How long does waterproofing last?
It depends on the system, the quality of the work, whether it is protected under tiles or exposed to the sun, and maintenance. Protected waterproofing usually lasts longer than exposed. Check the roof after each winter, keep the drains clear and repair any damaged spot early.
Can a bathroom be waterproofed without lifting the tiles?
Clear sealers and regrouting can reduce water getting through the joints, but they do not replace real waterproofing under the tiles. If water is reaching the ceiling below, the dependable fix is usually to lift the tiles, repair the pipes, waterproof again and flood test before tiling.
What is a flood test?
You block the drain, fill the bathroom or roof with water a few centimetres deep, leave it for 24 to 48 hours and check the ceiling below. If any damp appears, the waterproofing is repaired while it is still exposed. It is done before tiling, because a repair after tiling means breaking tiles.
Does roof insulation cut cooling costs?
Yes. It reduces the heat coming through the roof into the top floor, so the air conditioning works less and the flat stays cooler in summer. How much it saves depends on the insulation type and thickness, the finish colour and the roof’s exposure, so we do not put a number on it.
What does roof or bathroom waterproofing cost?
It depends on the area, the current state of the roof or bathroom, whether old layers or tiles have to come off, the waterproofing system, and the insulation and finish on top. We visit, measure and give you a written, itemised quotation.
