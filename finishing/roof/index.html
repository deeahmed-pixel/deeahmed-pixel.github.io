Roof terrace and penthouse finishing

A roof becomes either the best place in the house or the source of problems for everyone below it. The difference is the order: waterproofing, falls and drainage first, then floors, pergolas and seating.

Get a quotationWhatsApp

Waterproofing first: water and heat

Any roof finish starts with waterproofing and heat insulation, because a leak found after the tiles are down means lifting everything above it.

  1. Inspect the slab

    Check the slab, its cracks and where the drains are, and remove any old layers that are no longer sound.

  2. Falls

    A screed laid to gentle falls towards the drains, so water never stands anywhere on the roof.

  3. Waterproofing

    A continuous waterproofing layer over the whole roof, turned up the parapet walls and sealed carefully at the drains and around every pipe that comes through.

  4. Heat insulation

    Insulation boards that cut the heat reaching the floor below, which makes a real difference in summer.

  5. Flood test

    Before any tile, the roof is flooded and left for a day or two, and the ceiling below is checked.

  6. Protection layer

    A layer that protects the waterproofing from knocks and from the sun before the floor finish goes on.

The exact order of the layers depends on the waterproofing system used. What matters is that each layer is checked before the next one covers it.

Loads and drainage before decoration

Before you add large planters, a jacuzzi or new walls on a roof, a structural engineer should confirm the slab can carry the weight.

  • Wet soil in planters is heavy, and a filled jacuzzi weighs far more than it looks.
  • Brick walls and any new structure add a permanent load to the slab.
  • Drains must stay open and easy to clean, never buried under flooring or decoration.
  • Pergola and canopy posts are fixed with a detail that does not puncture the waterproofing, or are sealed carefully around it.
  • Water, drainage and power routes for an outdoor kitchen are decided before the waterproofing, not after.

Floors that can take the sun

The best roof floor is light in colour and slip-resistant, because direct sun heats dark colours and smooth surfaces are slippery when wet.

FloorStrengthsWatch out for
Textured natural stoneStays relatively cool underfoot and is not slipperySome stones stain and need a sealer
Outdoor porcelainHard, easy to clean, many coloursChoose an outdoor anti-slip grade; dark colours get hot
Timber deckWarm, natural lookNeeds regular oiling, and sun and water age it
WPC deckLess upkeep than timberSome dark boards get very hot in direct sun
GraniteVery hard and durablePolished granite is slippery; choose a flamed or textured finish

Pergolas: timber, steel or aluminium

No material wins in every case: the choice depends on the look, the upkeep you are willing to do, and how close you are to the sea.

PergolaStrengthsWeaknesses
TimberNatural and warmNeeds regular oiling or painting; sun and humidity age it
SteelStrong, spans wide openingsMust be hot-dip galvanised and well painted, especially by the sea
AluminiumLight and low-maintenanceProfiles must suit the spans and the wind
Aluminium with moving louvresControls sun and shade; some systems close against rainNeeds drainage for rainwater, precise installation and motor upkeep

Wind on a roof is stronger than at ground level, so any pergola or canopy needs fixings that are calculated, not just a few screws into the tiles.

Outdoor kitchen and seating

Outdoor kitchen tops on a roof must take direct sun, and granite suits better than quartz here, because quartz can change colour in strong sunlight.

  • A sink with a properly connected drain, and a supply valve that can be closed for winter.
  • Cabinets in outdoor-rated materials or steel and stainless steel, not indoor boards.
  • A barbecue placed so smoke does not blow towards the neighbours or the windows.
  • Built-in seating with storage underneath for cushions in winter.
  • Enough shade over the seating, because a roof without shade cannot be used by day in summer.

Electrics and outdoor lighting

Roof electrics should be treated as outdoor work: light fittings and sockets made for rain and dust, on circuits protected by an earth-leakage breaker.

  • Sockets with tight covers, away from where water collects.
  • Separate circuits for lighting, the outdoor kitchen and any jacuzzi.
  • Layered lighting: soft general light, light on paths and steps, and light for planting.
  • Spare conduit for speakers, a screen or cameras you may want later, instead of breaking the floor open again.

Additions and the rules

Building a room or any enclosed space on a roof is building work, and building work needs a licence from the relevant authority; in a compound, the developer's approval as well.

Even a fixed pergola or canopy may be restricted by compound rules or by the building's own agreement, so ask the management or the owners' association and get the answer in writing before the design starts.

Do not rely on anyone's word that something is "normally fine". Ask the authority or the management directly.

How Itmam finishes a roof

We visit the roof, check its condition and measure it, then write you an itemised quotation before any work starts, with the waterproofing, the floors, the pergola and the electrics listed as separate items.

Pergola frames, railings and other metalwork come from our own workshop, and granite and stone tops are templated, cut and installed by the same team.

On the North Coast we use the coastal specification: seaside-class powder coating, 316 stainless fixings and hot-dip galvanised steel.

Questions people ask

What waterproofing goes on a roof before finishing?
A continuous waterproofing layer over the whole roof and up the walls around it, heat insulation to protect the floor below, and correct falls to the drains. The type of system, whether membranes, coatings or another, depends on the roof's condition and what will sit on top. The most important step is a flood test before the tiles.
Timber, steel or aluminium pergola?
Timber looks warm but needs regular oiling; steel is strong and spans wide but must be hot-dip galvanised and painted; aluminium is light and needs little upkeep. By the sea, galvanised steel or aluminium with a seaside-class coating lasts best. Whatever you choose, it must be fixed properly against the wind.
Can I build a room on the roof?
Not without a licence. A room on a roof is building work that needs a permit from the relevant authority, the developer's approval in a compound, and a structural check that the slab can carry it. Ask the authority directly and keep the answer in writing.
What roof flooring stays cool?
Light-coloured, textured natural stone stays coolest underfoot and is not slippery. Light outdoor porcelain is a good second choice. Dark porcelain and some dark WPC boards get very hot in direct sun.
What sets the cost of a roof terrace?
The roof's current condition and the waterproofing it needs, the area, the floor finish, the pergola, and the outdoor kitchen and electrics. We do not give a figure before seeing it; we write an itemised quotation with quantities.
