Guides · Colour and paint

Wall paint colours and paint types: a guide to choosing

Calm wall paint colours such as off-white, vanilla latte and light grey are popular in Egyptian homes now, but the same colour looks different from one room to the next depending on the light, the floor and the type of paint. This guide covers the paint types used in Egypt, how to choose and test a colour, and what has to happen to the wall before any colour goes on.

Paint types in Egypt: which one goes where

Most walls in Egypt are painted today with plastic (emulsion) paint, which is water-based and comes in several sheens; oil paint belongs on wood and metal, and moisture-resistant paints in bathrooms and kitchens:

TypeLookSuitsWiping and washing
Matt emulsion (مطفي)No sheen; hides small flaws in the wallCeilings, bedrooms, reception roomsThe least, unless labelled washable
Semi-gloss emulsion (نص لمعة)A light sheenCorridors, children’s rooms, kitchensHigher than matt
Gloss emulsion (لامع)A clear shine that shows every rippleAreas wiped often, on an excellent wallThe highest of the emulsions
‘Satin’ and ‘velvet’ (ساتان، قطيفة)A soft sheen between matt and glossReception rooms, bedrooms, corridorsUsually washable, depending on the product
Oil / alkyd (زيت)A hard, glossy filmDoors, frames, metalwork, railingsVery high
Moisture- and mould-resistantUsually matt or semi-glossBathrooms and kitchensTakes wiping and steam

‘Satin’ and ‘velvet’ are names used in the market, not one specification. ‘Velvet’ usually has the quieter sheen and the softer feel, but that varies from maker to maker, so read the sheen level on the can and whether it says washable.

Oil paint is used less on walls now because it smells strongly, white oil paint tends to yellow with time, and its film does not let water vapour through the way emulsion does, so it blisters or peels on a damp wall. Washable emulsions now do its old job in kitchens and stairwells.

Calm wall paint colours: the shades popular now

Light, calm shades are the most popular in Egyptian homes now, because they are easy on the eye and make a space feel larger. But colour names change from one maker to another, so the colour code is the reference, not the name.

ColourCharacterWatch out for
Off-whiteA broken white with a warm or cool tintThere are many, so compare samples side by side
Vanilla latteA warm cream between white and beigeCan look too yellow in west-facing sun
BeigeWarm, from very pale to sandyNext to a floor of the same shade, the room looks flat
GreigeA grey leaning to beige, neither cold nor warmGreyer in cool light, beiger in sun
Light grey (رصاصي فاتح)A calm, contemporary greyCan look gloomy in a dim room, so choose one with a warm base
‘Breeze’ shadesA pale blue or green leaning to greyLooks colder than you expected in cool light

If you like a deeper colour, put it on one wall, behind the bed or on the television wall, and keep the other walls in a light shade of the same family; this is common in modern wall painting.

Colour never works alone: light, space and floor

The same colour changes from room to room with the light from the window and the colour of the floor and the wood:

  • North-facing rooms have steady but cooler light, so grey leans blue; warm shades such as vanilla and beige balance it. Many sea-facing flats in Alexandria face roughly this way.
  • West-facing rooms get strong, warm afternoon sun that makes warm colours yellower; light grey and greige handle it better. South-facing rooms get more sun through the day, and east-facing rooms are brightest in the morning.
  • Rooms on a light well, or lit artificially most of the time: a light shade with a warm base, tested under the room’s own lamps, since warm yellowish light warms beige and cool white light cools grey.
  • Small rooms: one light colour on the walls, with the ceiling the same or lighter, blurs the room’s edges so it feels larger.
  • High ceilings in older buildings, as in many Alexandria flats: a ceiling slightly darker than the walls, or the ceiling colour taken down the wall to the cornice line, makes the room feel warmer.
  • Floor and wood: choose the colour after them and hold the sample next to them. Beige floors and warm wood go with cream and vanilla, grey porcelain with light grey and greige, and dark wood with light walls that show it off.

Colours for each part of the home, from bedroom to stairwell

Each part of the home is used differently, so the colour and the type of paint change with it:

  • Bedrooms: calm, low-saturation shades in matt or velvet, because the side light of a bedside lamp shows ripples in any wall with a sheen. A common choice is a deeper wall behind the bed with the rest kept light.
  • Reception and living room: a neutral such as off-white or greige that ties open spaces together, and a washable soft sheen if there are children at home.
  • Kitchen and bathroom: a moisture- and mould-resistant paint that takes wiping, on the untiled walls and the ceiling, with an extractor fan venting outside.
  • Stairs, corridors and landings: a washable paint, ideally with a darker lower band up to about a metre, divided from the lighter wall above by a line or a moulding. A shared stairwell is agreed with the building management or the occupants’ union before choosing a colour.
  • Ceilings: white or lighter than the walls, in a matt paint that hides flaws under concealed lighting.

Test the colour on your wall before you buy

A small colour card or a photo on a phone is not enough, because a colour usually looks stronger across a whole wall, and each room’s light changes it:

  1. A large sample

    Paint an area of about A3 size (30 × 42 cm) in two coats, or paint a white board you can move, because a coloured wall underneath changes how the sample looks.

  2. On two walls

    One facing the window and one in shadow, and compare two or three close shades side by side.

  3. Morning and evening

    Look at it in the morning and at night under the room’s lighting, once it is fully dry, because wet paint looks different.

  4. Note the code and batch

    Record the product, the colour code, the sheen and the batch number, order each colour’s whole quantity at once, and keep a little for touch-ups.

Preparation decides how the paint looks

The finest colour will not hide a badly prepared wall. The usual order:

  1. Deal with damp first

    If the wall has stains, flaking or salts, find and fix the cause and let the wall dry, as the damp walls guide explains; then scrape off loose paint and fill the cracks.

  2. A sealer coat

    On new plaster and repaired patches: alkali-resistant on new plaster, and a bonding primer on glossy or oil-painted surfaces.

  3. Putty and sanding

    Two or three knife coats depending on how flat the wall is, sanded between coats and checked with a lamp held at a low angle, then a primer over the putty as the paint system requires.

  4. Two finish coats

    Two coats is normal; a third is usually needed for deep colours or when going from dark to light, and the time between coats follows the product’s instructions.

The higher the sheen, the better the wall must be: matt hides small ripples, while gloss shows every knife mark and every patch in raking light.

To work out the quantity: litres = area to paint × number of coats ÷ the coverage per litre printed on the can, and the paint calculator on our site works out the area and the litres for one room or a whole flat.

Humidity and sea air: painting in Alexandria and coastal cities

Itmam is based in Alexandria, where the air is humid most of the year and carries salt near the sea, and that changes the choice of paint in every coastal city:

  • Bathrooms and kitchens: mould-resistant paint with an extractor fan and daily airing, because anti-mould additives do not replace ventilation.
  • Do not paint over active damp: a sealing paint traps the water, so it spreads through the wall or the paint blisters.
  • Sea-facing walls: repair façade cracks and window sills first, because they let in moisture and salts, and inside use a paint that lets the wall breathe.
  • Drying is slower in humid weather, so do not cut the time between coats, and keep wardrobes a little away from cold outer walls so mould does not form behind them.
  • Façades are a different system: an alkali-resistant primer and a flexible exterior topcoat that copes with sun and salt, and changing their colour is a matter for the building management or the compound.

Questions people ask

Which wall paint colours are popular in 2026?
Light, calm shades are what is popular in Egyptian homes now: off-white, vanilla latte, beige, greige, light grey, and pale blue or green ‘breeze’ shades. But choose by your room’s light and floor, not by fashion, and test a large sample on the wall before you buy.
Light grey or beige: how do I choose?
By the light and the floor. A north-facing room has cool light, so beige and vanilla warm it up while grey can look gloomy. A west-facing room gets warm afternoon sun, which light grey and greige handle better. With grey porcelain, grey sits more easily; with beige marble and warm wood, beige does. If you are torn, greige sits in between.
Matt or gloss emulsion?
Matt for ceilings and most walls, because it hides the wall’s flaws; gloss takes more wiping but shows every ripple and patch. Semi-gloss and satin are a middle way for corridors, children’s rooms and kitchens. The higher the sheen, the better the wall preparation has to be.
What do ‘satin’ and ‘velvet’ paint mean?
Names used in the market for emulsions with a soft sheen between matt and gloss, usually washable. ‘Velvet’ normally has the quieter sheen and a soft feel, and ‘satin’ a slightly clearer sheen, but this varies from maker to maker. Read the can for the sheen level and whether it is washable, and look at a painted sample.
What colours suit a modern bedroom?
Calm, low-saturation shades: vanilla latte, pale beige, greige, light grey and breeze shades. A popular approach is a deeper wall behind the bed in the same family, with the rest kept light. Choose matt or velvet, because the side light of a bedside lamp shows any ripple in a wall with a sheen.
What paint should I use on a stairwell?
A washable paint that takes hands and bags, ideally with a darker lower band up to about a metre and a lighter colour above, divided by a line or a moulding. On an uneven wall, avoid full gloss. A shared building stairwell should be agreed with the occupants’ union or the building management before you choose the colour.
