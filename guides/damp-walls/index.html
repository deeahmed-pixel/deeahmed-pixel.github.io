Damp walls: find the cause first

Painting over damp hides it for a season, and then it comes back, usually worse. The fix depends on where the water comes from, and the shape of the stain usually tells you.

Get a quotationWhatsApp

What the damp is telling you

The height, place and season of a damp patch usually point to its cause, so look carefully before you scrape anything.

What you seeLikely causeFirst check
A band low on a ground-floor wall, with a tide mark and flaking paintRising damp from the groundOutside ground levels, drainage and any old damp-proof layer
A patch below a bathroom or kitchen, or along a pipe routeA leak from supply or drain pipes, or failed waterproofingThe pipe routes, the water meter and the flat above
Black spots in corners, behind wardrobes and on cold outer walls, worse in winterCondensationVentilation, drying clothes indoors, extractor fans
Damp on an outside wall after rain, or under the roofRain getting in through cracks, the façade or the roofCracks, window sills, roof waterproofing and rainwater pipes
White powder or crystals, and bubbling paintSalts carried by moistureWhere the water comes from, because the salts are a symptom
Wet patches around an air-conditioner's indoor unit or along its routeThe condensate drain leaking into the wallThe drain pipe and its fall

Condensation: water from the air inside

Condensation happens when warm, moist indoor air meets a cold wall and leaves water on it. It is the usual cause of black mould in closed rooms.

  • Cooking, showers and drying clothes indoors put a lot of water into the air, and Alexandria's air is humid already.
  • Extractor fans in the kitchen and bathroom that vent outside, and opening the windows daily, do more than any paint.
  • Keep wardrobes and sofas a little away from cold outer walls so that air can move behind them.
  • Clean mould with a suitable anti-mould wash, let the wall dry, then repaint with a breathable paint. An anti-mould additive helps, but it does not replace ventilation.
  • A dehumidifier helps in flats that stay closed for long periods.

Leaks: from your pipes or the neighbours'

A leak gives a patch that keeps growing or comes back in the same place, and it must be found and repaired before any work on the wall.

  • Close every tap and watch the water meter. If it still moves, there is a leak on the supply side.
  • Damp that grows when a bathroom is used points to a drain, a shower tray or failed floor waterproofing.
  • A plumber can pressure-test the pipes and use a moisture meter to trace the wet area without breaking open every wall.
  • If the water comes from the flat above, take dated photos, speak to the neighbour and the building's occupants' union, and agree the repair in writing. If that fails, take legal advice.
  • After the repair, let the wall dry fully before re-plastering or painting.

Waterproofing for bathrooms and roofs is covered on our waterproofing page.

Rising damp on ground floors

Rising damp is moisture from the ground drawn up into the base of the walls. It mainly affects ground floors and basements without a working damp-proof layer.

  • First make sure it really is rising damp: many cases that look like it are leaks, condensation, or outside ground that sits higher than the floor inside.
  • Lower the ground outside, or slope it away from the wall, and make sure rainwater and garden irrigation drain away from it.
  • Dense cement render and gloss paint at the base of a wall can trap moisture and push it higher, so they may need to come off.
  • A new damp-proof barrier, such as a chemical injection, is specialist work. Have a specialist assess the wall and explain the method before you agree to it.
  • Then re-plaster with a render suited to damp, salty walls, and finish with a breathable paint.

The white salt on the wall

The white powder or crystals on a damp wall are salts left behind as the water evaporates. They are a symptom of moisture, not a separate problem.

  • The salts can come from the bricks, the mortar, sand that was not washed, the ground, or sea air that settles on façades near the shore.
  • Brush them off dry. Washing them puts water back into the wall.
  • They return as long as water keeps moving through the wall, so stop the source first.
  • Old plaster that is full of salt usually has to come off and be replaced, or the new paint will blister again.

Before you paint: the right order

Repairing a damp wall is a sequence, and skipping a step is why damp comes back.

  1. Find the cause

    Use the signs above, with a plumber or a specialist where needed.

  2. Fix the source

    Repair the leak, improve the ventilation, correct the outside levels or treat the rising damp.

  3. Let the wall dry

    Drying takes time, longer for thick walls and in winter. Painting a wet wall traps the water.

  4. Remove what is damaged

    Scrape loose paint, brush off salts, and take off plaster that is soft, hollow or salty.

  5. Re-plaster

    Use a mortar suited to the wall and its exposure, made with clean, washed sand.

  6. Paint with a breathable system

    Use paint that lets the wall breathe. A sealing paint over a wall that is still wet only moves the damp somewhere else.

When to call a specialist

Call a specialist when the damp is structural, near the electrics or keeps coming back, because those cases need more than a painter.

  • Cracks that widen, or concrete that is flaking and showing rusty steel: have a structural engineer inspect it.
  • Damp near sockets, switches or the distribution board: switch off that circuit and call an electrician.
  • Mould spread over large areas, or anyone at home with breathing problems.
  • Damp that returns after a repair, or whose source is in another flat or a shared pipe.
  • Any plan to inject, tank or seal a wall: ask how the method works, and what happens if the damp comes back.

Questions people ask

What causes damp on a wall?
Usually one of four things: condensation from humid indoor air, a leak from pipes or a bathroom, moisture rising from the ground on lower floors, or rain getting in through an outside wall. The height, place and season of the patch usually tell you which.
Can I paint over damp with a sealing paint?
Not as a fix. A sealing paint over a wall that is still wet traps the water, which then spreads sideways, rises higher or blisters the paint. Find and fix the source, let the wall dry, and then use a paint system that lets the wall breathe.
How is ground-floor damp treated?
First confirm that it really is rising damp and not a leak or condensation. Then improve the ground levels and drainage outside, remove coatings that trap moisture, and have a specialist assess whether a new damp-proof barrier is needed, before re-plastering with a render suited to damp, salty walls.
What is the white salt on my wall?
Salts that water carried through the wall and left behind as it dried. They come from the bricks, the mortar, unwashed sand, the ground or sea air. Brush them off dry, stop the water, and replace plaster that is full of salt.
The damp comes from the neighbour's flat. What now?
Record it with dated photos, talk to the neighbour and the building's occupants' union, and agree in writing on the repair and who carries it out. Wait until the leak is fixed and the wall has dried before repairing your side. If you cannot agree, take legal advice.
Why is there black mould in the corners?
Usually condensation: moist indoor air meeting a cold corner or outer wall. Improve ventilation and extraction, keep furniture off cold walls, clean the mould with an anti-mould wash, and repaint with a breathable paint once the wall is dry.
