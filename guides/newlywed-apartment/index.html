Finishing a newly-weds’ flat: what comes first and what can wait

Finishing a newly-weds’ flat in 2026 begins with the things you will never see: the electrics, plumbing, waterproofing and air-conditioning pipework. Here are the priorities, a room-by-room checklist, calm colour schemes, ideas for a small flat, and the order and timing of the work before the wedding, with no prices.

Get a quotationWhatsApp

What to spend on first: the things you can’t change without breaking

Spend first on everything buried in the walls and floors, because changing it after you move in means breaking out, dust and refinishing; leave for later what can be changed with a screwdriver and a ladder.

ItemGet it right from the startWhy now
ElectricsThe meter’s capacity, from the electricity company; a board with enough breakers; a separate circuit for each air conditioner, the water heater and the oven; enough sockets where they will be usedAdding one after painting means cutting the wall or a visible cable
Plumbing and waterproofingSupply and drain pipes with proper falls; bathroom and kitchen floors waterproofed and turned up the walls; a flood test before tilingLeaks show up after you move in, sometimes in the ceiling of the flat below
Air-conditioning first fixCopper pipes, a drain line with a steady fall and a power supply to every room you will cool, even if the units are bought laterBuried pipework cannot be added after gypsum and paint without breaking out
Door framesPlumb, and set to the finished floor levelA leaning frame shows on every door, and fixing it means taking it out
Floor levelsOne level between rooms with no thresholds, and a fall towards the floor drain in the bathroom, kitchen and balconyA raised threshold or standing water stays with the flat

Chandeliers and light fittings, as long as their points are ready, wallpaper, light decoration, curtains and some furniture can start simple and be upgraded after the wedding without breaking anything. If the budget is tight, save on what shows, not on what is buried.

A finishing checklist for a newly-weds’ flat, room by room

Write the finishing list room by room before any tradesman starts, with the positions of the appliances and furniture, because they decide where every socket and water point goes.

  • Kitchen: the appliance sizes before the first fix; a water and drain point for every appliance that needs one; a socket for each fixed appliance and sockets above the worktop; and an extractor outlet to the outside. Changes to the gas line or the cooker position go through the gas company.
  • Bathroom: waterproofing and a flood test before tiling, a fall to the floor drain, an extractor fan or a window that really opens, and an earth-leakage breaker on its circuits. A gas water heater must not go inside a closed bathroom.
  • Bedroom: a socket and a light point on each side of the bed, a light switch at the door and another by the bed, an air-conditioning point, and a curtain pocket in the ceiling if there is gypsum board. Fix the wardrobe position before the lighting is laid out.
  • Reception: a TV wall with a socket, internet and satellite points and an empty conduit for the cables, lighting on more than one circuit, and an air-conditioning point.
  • Balcony: a fall towards the drain, a slip-resistant floor suited to the outdoors, and a weatherproof socket. Ask the building management or compound where the outdoor air-conditioning unit may go, and about enclosing the balcony.
  • Entrance: the distribution board somewhere easy to reach, and a point for the internet router roughly in the middle of the flat.

The furniture list itself is on the furnishing page; this guide is about the finishing.

Calm colours for a newly-weds’ flat: how to choose

The calm schemes newly-weds usually ask for rest on one light base colour for the whole flat and a single accent in one chosen place, not a different colour in every room.

  • Warm white with beige: calm and easy to live with, and it goes with light wood and cream marble.
  • Sandy beige with walnut or oak: warmer, and it suits the reception and the bedroom.
  • Light grey leaning to beige, with white: a calm modern look, with a few black touches in the handles and light fittings.
  • An accent on one wall: a light olive green, a grey-blue or a muted pink, behind the bed or behind the sofa.

Try a sample of each colour on the wall itself in morning light and at night, because colour changes with the light. Rooms with little sun look cooler, so a warm white usually suits them. Choose a washable paint in the kitchen and corridor; the wall paint colours guide covers the shades in detail.

Ideas for finishing a small flat so it feels bigger

A small flat grows through decisions, not demolition: light colours, reflected light, storage you cannot see, and doors that need no space to open.

  • One light colour for walls and ceiling, and one floor through the reception, bedrooms and corridor, with no thresholds.
  • Mirrors that reflect daylight, in the entrance or beside a window, and mirrored wardrobe doors.
  • Hidden storage: wardrobes up to the ceiling, a bed with storage underneath, cabinets above the corridor door, and a niche in a wall that is not load-bearing and has no pipes or cables in it.
  • Sliding doors for the wardrobe and the kitchen. A door that slides into the wall needs a frame built into the wall before plastering, and a stretch of wall with no switches or pipes, so decide early.
  • Lighting on more than one level: a perimeter cove and wall lights instead of one central chandelier, without a full false ceiling that eats into the height.
  • Plain fronts with recessed handles, in a colour close to the wall, so the wardrobes disappear into the room. That is the heart of a simple modern finish.

The order of works, and who to talk to before you start

The right order is simple: everything that breaks into walls and floors comes before everything that decorates them, and decisions on paper come before any tradesman.

  1. Decisions on paper

    The room layout, the kitchen and bathroom, and where the bed, TV, air conditioners and sockets go.

  2. Permission and coordination

    Ask the building management or compound about working hours, the lift, debris removal, the façade rules and outdoor air-conditioning units. Changes to the gas line or the position of the cooker or water heater go through the gas company, and big increases in electrical load through the electricity company.

  3. Walls and door frames

    Partitions and openings, and the door frames fixed to the finished floor level.

  4. First fix

    Electrics, plumbing and air conditioning, with a pressure test on the pipes and photos of the walls before they are closed.

  5. Plaster and waterproofing

    Plastering, then waterproofing the bathroom and kitchen, and the flood test.

  6. Floors and wall tiles

    Floor tiling and the bathroom and kitchen walls, then gypsum board and the first coats of paint.

  7. Fitted pieces

    The kitchen, doors, wardrobes, bathroom vanity and stone tops, after the final measurements.

  8. Final coat and handover, then furniture

    The last coat of paint, the clean, a room-by-room check against the quotation, and then the furniture goes in.

This coordination is practical advice, not a statement of law: the rules differ from place to place, so get the answer in writing. The finishing steps guide covers each stage in detail.

Timing before the wedding: start early, furnish last

Start as early as you can, because how long the finishing takes depends on the state of the flat, the amount of work and how quickly you decide, not on the wedding date.

  • Settle the kitchen, bathroom, floors and colours before work starts; late decisions are among the commonest causes of delay and breaking out.
  • Choose the tiles, sanitaryware and stone early, and buy all the tiles from one batch.
  • The made-to-measure kitchen, wardrobes and doors are measured once the walls and floors are finished, not from the drawing, and then made.
  • New plaster has to dry before it is painted, and that stage cannot be rushed.
  • Leave a gap between the end of the work and the wedding for the clean, fixing snags and bringing in the furniture.
  • If time is short, finish what is buried, the kitchen, the bathroom and the bedroom first, and leave decoration and decorative gypsum for later.

Questions people ask

How much does it cost to finish a newly-weds’ flat?
We don’t print a figure, because material prices change and a number given without a visit misleads. What decides the total: the state of the flat (red brick, semi-finished, or finished and being renewed), its net area, the number of bathrooms, the kitchen, the floors, the number of air conditioners, and the gypsum and lighting. We visit, measure and give you a written quotation, itemised with quantities.
What should we do first if the budget is tight?
Start with what is buried: the electrics, plumbing, waterproofing, air-conditioning first fix, door frames and floor levels, because none of them can be changed later without breaking out. Save on what shows: simple light fittings now that you replace later, less decoration, and furniture completed in stages.
Which calm colours suit a newly-weds’ flat in 2026?
One light base colour for the whole flat, such as warm white, beige or a light grey leaning to beige, and a single accent on one wall, such as a light olive green, a grey-blue or a muted pink. Try the sample on the wall in morning light and at night before you decide.
How do I finish a small flat so it feels bigger?
One light colour for walls and ceiling, one floor with no thresholds, mirrors that reflect the window’s light, wardrobes to the ceiling with plain fronts, and sliding doors for the wardrobe and kitchen. A door that slides into the wall must be decided before plastering, because it needs a frame built into the wall.
How long before the wedding should we start the finishing?
There is no single figure that fits every flat: the time depends on the state of the flat, the amount of work and how quickly you decide. Start early, settle the kitchen, bathroom and floors before work begins, write the time for each stage into the contract, and leave a gap before the wedding for the clean, the snags and the furniture.
What does a simple but stylish finish look like?
Simple means fewer materials and calmer colours, not weaker groundwork. One floor through the flat, two or three colours at most, lighting on more than one circuit, wardrobes to the ceiling with plain fronts, and a bathroom in easy-clean materials. Style comes from straight lines and neat detailing more than from the type of material.
