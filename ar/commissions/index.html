قطع منحوتة

Send us the picture.
We will tell you how it is made.

People send us a photograph of a stone piece and ask whether we can make it. The answer is almost always yes. The useful part of the answer is how — carved from one block, folded from a mitred slab, or stacked from waterjet segments around a hidden core. That decision sets the price, the lead time, and which stone will survive it.

How to read this page

Every piece here is drawn,
not photographed.

That is deliberate. A catalogue of commissions is a list of what we can make, and if we filled it with finished pieces lifted off the internet we would be showing you somebody else's work with our name under it. So each commission is a drawing and a construction method. Where we have already built the piece, our own photograph of our own job sits on that page, clearly marked.

  • Drawn, not borrowed
  • Built in our own workshop
  • Egyptian marble & granite
  • Waterjet, five-axis CNC and hand finishing
  • Priced from your drawing or ours

The honest part

What we will talk you out of.

Marble bowls in a children's bathroom. Polished treads by a pool. A carved bath on a first floor we have not surveyed. Onyx on a bar. A book-match decided after the block has been cut. We would rather lose the line than build something that looks wrong in a year.
